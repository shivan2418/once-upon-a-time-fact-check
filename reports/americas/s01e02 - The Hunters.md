# Once Upon a Time... The Americas — s01e02: The Hunters
As the Ice Age glaciers melt, the descendants of the Siberian migrants spread across North America. One band adopts the spear-thrower and the Clovis spearhead. A greedy group splits off to hunt for its own sake, and the great Ice Age animals disappear. Later the survivors turn to plants, invent the bow and build a respectful bison-hunting culture. A closing narration jumps to the 19th-century slaughter of the bison.

## Outdated (since 1991)
- **Quote:** "across siberia people arrive here in what we call montana"
  **Today:** The episode has people arriving overland east of the Rockies (Montana), which means a trip down the ice-free corridor between the ice sheets. Studies of plant and animal remains show the corridor could not support travellers until about 12,600 years ago (Pedersen et al., *Nature*, 2016). Older sites south of the ice are now accepted: Monte Verde in Chile (about 14,500 years, accepted 1997), Buttermilk Creek in Texas (about 15,500 years, 2011) and the White Sands footprints in New Mexico (about 21,000–23,000 years, 2021, confirmed 2023). Most researchers now think the first people came down the Pacific coast. To the episode's credit, its date of about 15,000 years ago (see the mishearing note below) is earlier than the Clovis-first dates usual in 1991 and fits the modern picture better.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "In 60 million buffalo, but by the end of the 19th century, only a few hundred were still alive."
  **Today:** The 60 million figure comes from Ernest Thompson Seton (1909). Studies of how many animals the grasslands could feed (Dan Flores, 1991; Andrew Isenberg, 2000) put the Great Plains at about 25–30 million, fewer in droughts. The low point is about right: William Hornaday's 1889 count found about 1,000 bison in all, only a few hundred of them wild in the United States.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "as the white hunters deliberately exterminated them to deprive the Indians"
  **Today:** The killing was real and catastrophic, and some army officers and politicians welcomed it as a way to force Plains nations onto reservations. But Flores (1991) and Isenberg (2000) showed that the main driver was the commercial hide trade, which boomed after a new tanning process (1871) made bison leather valuable for industrial belting. Other factors were drought, competition from horses and cattle, cattle diseases, and Native hunting for the robe trade, which had been thinning herds since the 1840s. The slaughter was not mainly a planned policy, and the Sheridan "let them kill" speech often quoted for it is poorly sourced.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- "about 15 million years before our modern era" is almost certainly a transcription error for 15,000 years. Glaciers were melting fast then, although the Ice Age formally ended about 11,700 years ago.
- The spread over generations "across the Great Plains as far as Nevada", the Great Lakes and the eastern woodlands. Nevada is in the Great Basin, not the Plains.
- The Clovis point near Clovis, New Mexico. Blackwater Draw is the type site, and Clovis is dated to about 13,000–12,700 years ago (Waters & Stafford 2007). Where the point was first made is unknown, and the show rightly says it took great skill to make.
- The spear-thrower (atlatl) shown as a new invention that throws farther. It was used with Clovis points, but it is older (Upper Palaeolithic Europe, about 20,000 years ago) and probably came with the first migrants.
- Driving herds over a cliff ("Massacre. Some got away"): bison jumps are well documented (Olsen-Chubbuck, about 10,000 years ago; Head-Smashed-In, from about 6,000 years ago), but they belong to slightly later bison hunters, not the mammoth era.
- The extinction of the great sloth, horses, camels and giant armadillos (glyptodonts) after over-hunting. Paul Martin's "overkill" hypothesis was prominent in 1991, and the debate continues today. Most researchers blame a mix of human hunting and rapid climate change (the Younger Dryas). Ancient DNA from sediments (Murchie et al. 2021) suggests horses and mammoths survived in the Yukon a few thousand years longer than once thought. "Disappeared the dun and the buffalo" is likely a mishearing (probably the mammoth and the giant Ice Age bison, since the next lines say "we still have our old friend the buffalo").
- Plant gathering and a broader diet after the big game declined matches the Archaic period.
- The bow "invented" after the megafauna disappeared: the episode gives no date. In fact the bow came from the north and reached the Plains only about 2,000–1,500 years ago.
- The bison prayer and the use of hide, horn, sinew and bone: broadly true of Plains peoples. The "wise elder who forbids waste" theme is a moral lesson. Kill sites show that ancient hunters sometimes wasted many animals.
