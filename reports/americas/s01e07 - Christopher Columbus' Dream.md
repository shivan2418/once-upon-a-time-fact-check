# Once Upon a Time... The Americas — s01e07: Christopher Columbus' Dream
The episode (in French) follows two lives side by side from 1466. Isabella of Castile marries Ferdinand of Aragon against her brother Henry IV's wishes and becomes queen in 1474. Columbus becomes a sailor, settles in Portugal and the Madeira islands, and comes to believe Asia can be reached by sailing west. After Portugal turns him down, he spends years petitioning the Spanish monarchs through commissions and the war against Granada, until Luis de Santángel's intervention wins him the 1492 contract. The transcript ends at the signing.

## Outdated (since 1991)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Comment pouvez-vous laisser l'obscurantisme, les manigances, avoir raison de mon ami Colom" [How can you let obscurantism and scheming get the better of my friend Columbus]
  **Today:** The experts who turned Columbus down in Portugal (1484–85) and Spain (1490) were not obscurantists. They were right that his distances were far too small. Columbus put Japan about 2,400 nautical miles (roughly 4,000–4,500 km) west of the Canaries, as in the episode's "pas plus de 4000 km" [no more than 4,000 km]. The real distance is about 19,000 km by that route, and his ships would have run out of supplies if America had not been in the way. The episode shows only his side of the argument.
  **Why it was believed:** Washington Irving's *Life of Columbus* (1828) cast the commissions as ignorant clerics opposing a visionary, and that picture lasted in schoolbooks. Specialists (e.g. Morison, 1942; Jeffrey Burton Russell, *Inventing the Flat Earth*, 1991) had shown that the dispute was about distance.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major

## Wrong (even in 1991)
- **Quote:** "En même année, une princesse de 16 ans, Isabelle, tirée de son couvent, débarque à la cour d'Espagne à Madrid où règne son frère Henri IV de Castille." [The same year, a 16-year-old princess, Isabella, taken from her convent, arrives at the court of Spain in Madrid, where her brother Henry IV of Castile reigns.]
  **Today:** Isabella was born on 22 April 1451, so in 1466 she turned 15, the same age as Columbus. She had not been raised in a convent. She grew up with her mother at the castle of Arévalo, and Henry IV brought her to his travelling court in about 1461–62, when she was ten. Henry was king of Castile; there was no "court of Spain" yet.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Il arrive que le hasard fasse bien les choses, alors que Vilhena se rendait à la cérémonie du mariage." [Sometimes chance works out well: as Villena was on his way to the wedding ceremony.]
  **Today:** The man who died on his way to marry Isabella (spring 1466) was Pedro Girón, Master of Calatrava, the **brother** of Juan Pacheco, Marquis of Villena. Villena himself lived until October 1474, as the episode later says ("Vilena va mourir" [Villena will die]). Girón probably died of an illness, though poison was rumoured. The narration names the wrong man, unless this is a mishearing.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Frère Pérez, informez-vous bien et revenez tout me dire sur ces princes." [Brother Pérez, find out all you can and come back and tell me everything about these princes.]
  **Today:** The anecdote is real, but the envoy was her chaplain **Alonso de Coca**. He reported that Charles of France was feeble, with thin legs and watery eyes, and that Ferdinand was handsome and spirited. Fray Juan Pérez was the La Rábida friar who later helped Columbus; the episode seems to have merged the two.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Je vais aller au Flandre. Malheureusement, une tempête nous a déportés vers l'ouest." [I was going to Flanders. Unfortunately, a storm drove us west.]
  **Today:** This is the legend of the "unknown pilot" (named here as Alonso de Huelva, i.e. Alonso Sánchez de Huelva). He was supposedly blown across the Atlantic and told Columbus of the lands before dying. The story first appears decades after 1492 (Oviedo, 1535, who called it a rumour; Garcilaso de la Vega, 1609). It was used to belittle Columbus's claims and has no contemporary evidence. The episode presents it as fact.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "Fais-ce comme une femme ce royaume que tu n'as pas à se défendre comme un homme." [(Weep) like a woman for this kingdom you could not defend like a man.]
  **Today:** The rebuke from Boabdil's mother as he looked back at Granada (the "Moor's Last Sigh") is a legend. It first appears in Antonio de Guevara's writings in the 1530s, about 40 years later. The episode presents it as a real scene.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- Columbus at sea as a boy of about 15 in 1466 matches his own claim that he started sailing at 14 (born 1451). He sailed mainly in Genoese waters, though, and "galion" is an anachronism: galleons appeared in the 16th century.
- The suitors Ferdinand of Aragon (her second cousin) and Charles of France (Duke of Berry/Guyenne, brother of Louis XI). The main rival candidate pushed by Henry and Villena was Afonso V of Portugal, whom the episode leaves out.
- Alfonso's death (July 1468) made Isabella heir two years later. She was then at Ávila (convent of Santa Ana), not Segovia.
- Isabella slipping away to marry Ferdinand at Valladolid in October 1469, with the Archbishop of Toledo (Alfonso Carrillo) officiating.
- Villena and Henry IV both died in 1474, and Isabella (23) and Ferdinand (22) took the throne, of **Castile**. Ferdinand became king of Aragon only in 1479.
- The French attack off Portugal: the battle of 13 August 1476 off Cape St Vincent, after which Columbus swam ashore at Lagos. It was near the Algarve, not Lisbon.
- Porto Santo: the governorship of the Perestrelo family (his wife Filipa's father, then her brother), the rabbits that overran the island (a story going back to Zurara and Cadamosto), and his son Diego.
- Carved wood drifting from the west: Ferdinand Columbus's biography reports such finds by Martín Vicente ("Vincent") and by Pedro Correa on Porto Santo.
- Toscanelli's map and letter (1474). The Florentine was a physician and astronomer. His own distance (about 5,000 nautical miles from Lisbon to Quinsay) was larger than Columbus's later figure.
- John II of Portugal turning Columbus down (around 1484–85). The demands (admiral, viceroy, governor, a tenth of the profits and golden spurs, i.e. knighthood) match the sources.
- La Rábida (1485), asking for bread and water for his son, Fray Juan Pérez ("Péresse") and Antonio de Marchena, correctly shown as two people. Pierre d'Ailly's *Imago Mundi* ("cardinal Dehi" is a mishearing), Aristotle, Seneca and Posidonius were among his authorities.
- Hernando de Talavera, the queen's confessor; Cardinal Mendoza; Columbus's first audience with the monarchs in 1486.
- Pedro de Arana and his sister Beatriz (Beatriz Enríquez de Arana, later the mother of Columbus's son Fernando). The rescue from bandits is invented drama.
- Commission after commission (1486–90) while the war went on; Málaga fell in August 1487; the plan was rejected; Columbus planned to try France.
- The antipodes objection: to its credit, the episode avoids the flat-earth myth. The clerics' objection is about whether people could live on the far side of a round earth (St Augustine, Nicholas of Lyra). Ferdinand Columbus's biography does report that the commission cited Augustine on the antipodes.
- The surrender of Granada (January 1492) on generous terms (mosques kept open, property kept) and Boabdil given lands in the Alpujarras. The episode does not mention that these terms were broken by forced conversions in 1499–1502.
- Pérez going to the queen, Luis de Santángel's offer to advance the money, and the Capitulations of Santa Fe (17 April 1492), drawn up by the secretary Juan de Coloma ("Juan de Colonna"). These included the key phrase about lands "discovered and to be discovered".
