# Once Upon a Time... Man — s01e14: The Quattrocento
The episode presents Renaissance Florence through the life of Leonardo da Vinci, from his apprenticeship with Verrocchio through Milan, Florence and Rome to his death in France in 1519. Along the way come Lorenzo de' Medici, the Pazzi conspiracy, Savonarola, Michelangelo, Raphael and Copernicus, plus Leonardo's inventions and his reasoning about fossils.

## Outdated (since 1978)
- **Quote:** "The parachute, and then the bicycle, propelled by a cogged wheel and chain. It would take us four more centuries to produce another one like it."
  **Today:** The bicycle sketch in the Codex Atlanticus came to light after the codex was restored in the 1960s and was published as Leonardo's in 1974. Since Hans-Erhard Lessing's study (1997), most historians think it is a modern addition or forgery and not Leonardo's work. (The parachute drawing is real.)
  **Confidence:** high

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Struck by his pupil's beauty, Verrocchio makes a decision. His David will have young Leonardo's face."
  **Today:** This is a guess, not a fact. No document says Leonardo was the model for Verrocchio's bronze David, and its date (c. 1466–76) is itself uncertain.
  **Why it was believed:** The idea spread in 20th-century art writing and popular biographies because it is appealing. It has never been confirmed.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Overwhelmed by his pupil's talent, Verrocchio renounced painting and from then on did nothing but sculpture."
  **Today:** This is Giorgio Vasari's anecdote (1550) about the angel Leonardo painted in the *Baptism of Christ*. Verrocchio's workshop went on producing paintings (for example the Pistoia altarpiece, with Lorenzo di Credi), so historians treat the story as a legend.
  **Why it was believed:** Vasari's *Lives* was the standard source for centuries, and this story was repeated in almost every popular life of Leonardo.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "In the year 1492, Lorenzo the Magnificent passed away without the blessing of Savonarola"
  **Today:** The story that Savonarola refused Lorenzo absolution comes from Savonarola's later supporters. Poliziano was at the deathbed, and his letter says Savonarola gave Lorenzo his blessing. Historians regard the refusal as a pro-Savonarola legend.
  **Why it was believed:** It is a dramatic story, and it appears in many 19th- and 20th-century popular histories.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1978)
- **Quote:** "Pythagoras' work was brought back to life, man the measure of all things."
  **Today:** "Man is the measure of all things" is the saying of **Protagoras**, not Pythagoras. This may be a Whisper mishearing or a dub slip.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "In 1510, Copernicus decided to wait not nine years as Pythagoras recommended, but four times nine years"
  **Today:** In his preface, Copernicus does say he held his book back for "four times nine years", but the nine-year rule is the poet **Horace's** advice (*Ars Poetica*). He mentions the Pythagoreans separately, for their habit of keeping teachings secret.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "And so da Vinci went to France, without his adopted son."
  **Today:** Salaì went to France with Leonardo in 1516, alongside Francesco Melzi. French royal accounts record payments to him there before he returned to Milan around 1518. (He was Leonardo's assistant, not a legally adopted son.)
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "...he died in 1519. Cortes had reached Mexico, Magellan was halfway around the world, Charles V had just been crowned emperor."
  **Today:** Leonardo died on 2 May 1519. Magellan only left Spain in August–September 1519. Charles was **elected** emperor in June 1519 and crowned only in 1520 (Aachen) and 1530 (by the Pope). Only the Cortés part fits.
  **Confidence:** high

## Simplified but fine
- Lorenzo de' Medici ruling from 1469; the alum dispute and sack of Volterra (1472); the Pazzi conspiracy (1478, backed by Pope Sixtus IV) and Leonardo's sketch of the hanged Bernardo Bandini.
- Leonardo's letter to Ludovico Sforza offering military engines, covered "tanks" and artistic services; the Sforza horse; Cecilia Gallerani.
- Savonarola's bonfires and death in 1498, the same year da Gama reached India; 1492 events (Granada, Columbus, Alexander VI).
- Copernicus graduating from Ferrara in 1503; the Battle of Anghiari; the 1504 committee deciding where to put Michelangelo's David.
- St Peter's begun in 1506 under Bramante (transcribed "Vermont"); the Sistine ceiling from 1508 to 1512; Plato in *The School of Athens* traditionally modelled on Leonardo.
- Leonardo's argument from fossils in layers against the Flood: accurate (Codex Leicester).
- Francis I's victory at Marignano (1515) and invitation to Leonardo; the Mona Lisa going to the French crown (the scene of the king trying to buy it is dramatised).
- Greek scholars fleeing Constantinople and the printing press helping spread humanism.
