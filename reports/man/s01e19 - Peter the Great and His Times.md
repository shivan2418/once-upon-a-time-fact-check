# Once Upon a Time... Man — s01e19: Peter the Great and His Times
The episode follows the young Peter the Great from the Voronezh shipyards and the taking of Azov to his Grand Embassy to western Europe, the crushing of the Streltsy and his ban on beards. It covers the Great Northern War against Charles XII and the building of St Petersburg. It ends with the rise of Prussia under Frederick I and the "soldier king" Frederick William I, with his forced army recruiting.

## Outdated (since 1978)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "at that time russia was one of the most backward countries not one scientist thinker or philosopher not one geometrist an illiterate church..."
  **Today:** Muscovy was poorer and less literate than western Europe, but it was not an intellectual desert. Western influences were already strong in the later 1600s through the Foreign Quarter of Moscow, Kievan scholars such as Simeon Polotsky, reformers such as Vasily Golitsyn and Ordin-Nashchokin, and the Slavo-Greco-Latin Academy (1687). Historians such as Lindsey Hughes stress that Peter sped up changes that had already begun.
  **Why it was believed:** Peter's own propaganda, and the 18th–19th-century "Westerniser" story, cast him as single-handedly dragging a dark Russia into Europe.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1978)
- **Quote:** "voronezh on the don 700 kilometers from moscow"
  **Today:** Voronezh is about 470 km from Moscow in a straight line and a little over 500 km by road.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "May 1697, Peter I leaves Russia for the West"
  **Today:** The Grand Embassy left Moscow in March 1697. Peter travelled with it incognito.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- Peter aged 17 in 1689 and his foreign friends: the Scot Patrick Gordon and the Swiss Franz Lefort.
- The failed attack on Azov in 1695 (Peter was 23), the new fleet built at Voronezh, and Azov taken from the sea in 1696.
- Learning shipbuilding and navigation from Dutch teachers and soldiering from a Scot. His geometry tutor, Franz Timmerman, was also Dutch, not from Hamburg.
- Working in a Dutch shipyard and studying navigation in England, then hurrying home over the Streltsy revolt of 1698. The Streltsy had killed members of his mother's family in 1682, when he was nearly 10.
- The 1698 ban on beards and long robes, with a beard tax from 1705, and his etiquette rules (*The Honourable Mirror of Youth*, 1717).
- Charles XII (aged 18 in 1700) beating the Danes, then the Russians at Narva against heavy odds. The Swedes numbered about 10,000, not 8,000, against 35,000–40,000.
- Charles's long campaign in Poland against Augustus II, the founding of St Petersburg in 1703 and the Swedish army ruined by the winter of 1708–09 (Poltava is not named).
- Russia becoming the leading Baltic power, so that "nothing was left of Christoph Adolf's legacy" (Gustavus Adolphus).
- Frederick I becoming "King in Prussia" in 1701. "1913" is a mishearing of 1713, when Frederick William I succeeded.
- The "soldier king" hoarding his army. He did fight once, against Sweden in Pomerania in 1715. His recruiters' tricks and his regiment of giants at Potsdam are well documented.
