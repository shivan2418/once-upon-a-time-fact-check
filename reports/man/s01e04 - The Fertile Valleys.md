# Once Upon a Time... Man — s01e04: The Fertile Valleys
The episode covers the start of farming and the Neolithic, megaliths at Carnac, early towns, and Mohenjo-daro on the Indus. It shows a Mesopotamian flood leading to shared canal-building and the first writing, then pyramid-building and Ramses II in Egypt. It ends with the Exodus and Old Testament episodes (Jericho, Samson, David and Goliath, Solomon) and the idea of one God.

## Outdated (since 1978)
- **Quote:** "A daily prison for more than a hundred thousand men."
  **Today:** The figure of 100,000 workers comes from Herodotus. Excavations of the Giza workers' town and cemeteries (Lehner and Hawass, from the 1990s) and the Wadi al-Jarf papyri (found in 2013, including the diary of the official Merer) show a workforce of perhaps 20,000–30,000. Many were rotating teams of labourers who were housed, fed and given medical care. They were not slaves or prisoners.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "Pharaoh had no choice but to let the Israelites go, for they had a rendezvous with destiny."
  **Today:** Here the Exodus is told as history and placed under Ramses II. In 1978 many scholars in the Albright tradition still thought a historical Exodus was likely. Decades of fieldwork in Sinai and Canaan have found no trace of it, and most historians now treat it as a founding tradition, not an event that can be documented (for example Finkelstein and Silberman, *The Bible Unearthed*, 2001).
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Jericho Seven times the walls will tumble down"
  **Today:** Jericho had no walled town for Joshua to conquer in the 13th century BC. Its Bronze Age walls were destroyed around 1550 BC, and the site was then almost empty.
  **Why it was believed:** In the 1930s John Garstang claimed to have found Joshua's collapsed walls, and Bible-history books kept repeating this. Kathleen Kenyon's excavations (1952–58) had already disproved it for specialists.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "For the first time, men heard these words, Thou shalt not kill, thou shalt not covet. And men heard for the first time about one God."
  **Today:** Laws against killing are much older: the Code of Ur-Nammu (about 2100 BC) and Hammurabi's laws (about 1750 BC) come centuries earlier. The pharaoh Akhenaten's worship of a single god, the Aten (about 1350 BC), also comes before any likely date for Moses. Many scholars think strict Israelite monotheism took shape only in the 7th–6th centuries BC.
  **Why it was believed:** Western school and Sunday-school tradition taught the Ten Commandments and Mosaic monotheism as humanity's moral "first".
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major

## Wrong (even in 1978)
- **Quote:** "5,000 years before Christ, Mesopotamia, where major cities flourished, Jarmo in Iraq, Jericho in Palestine, and in Turkey, Shatal Hayyuk. These highly evolved peoples already knew how to alloy metals."
  **Today:** "Shatal Hayyuk" is a mishearing of Çatalhöyük. Jericho and Çatalhöyük are not in Mesopotamia. All three were farming villages or towns, not "major cities", and all are older than 5000 BC: Jericho goes back to about 9000 BC, and Jarmo and Çatalhöyük to about 7000 BC. True cities came with Uruk, around 3500 BC. These sites worked native copper, but bronze-making (alloying) came later.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Roadways are completely paved."
  **Today:** The streets of Mohenjo-daro were mostly beaten earth. Its famous feature was covered brick drains running beside them. The city's grid layout, brick houses and wheeled carts are correctly described.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "along the river Nile, 2,700 years before our time" / "Thirteen hundred years before our time ruled Ramses II"
  **Today:** These should be 2700 BC (the pyramid age) and about 1279–1213 BC (Ramses II). "Before our time" (meaning before today) is almost certainly a dub slip for "before our era" (BC). As spoken, it would put the pyramids about 700 BC.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "A thousand blocks of stone, some weighing two tons."
  **Today:** The Great Pyramid alone has about 2.3 million blocks averaging about 2.5 tonnes, and some granite beams weigh 50–80 tonnes. "A thousand" is probably a translation slip for "thousands".
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "In later centuries came invasions by the Hyksos, war with the Hittites"
  **Today:** The Hyksos ruled in about 1650–1550 BC, before Ramses II, not after him. The great war with the Hittites (the Battle of Kadesh, 1274 BC) happened during Ramses II's own reign.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Ten centuries later, in A.D. 30, Cleopatra would choose suicide over surrender to Augustus"
  **Today:** Cleopatra died in 30 BC, not AD 30. This is probably a dub slip.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- Farming started after the ice age, when the big game was gone, and the Mesolithic gave way to the Neolithic (polished stone): fine.
- The Carnac megaliths in Brittany belong to prehistory: fine (about 4500–3300 BC).
- Shared canal and dike work along Mesopotamian rivers led to organised society: a classic and reasonable simplification.
- Writing began with pictographs on clay tablets: right. In reality the first texts were bookkeeping, not stories.
- A local river flood remembered as the "Great Deluge" is a reasonable hypothesis (flood layers at Ur, Shuruppak and elsewhere).
- Ramses II as the great builder-pharaoh, tax collectors taking the harvest, and Shelley's "Look upon my works" line (*Ozymandias*, a poem about Ramses II): fine.
- Cleopatra's death ending about 30 centuries of pharaonic history: fine.
