# Once Upon a Time... Man — s01e01: And Earth Was Created
The episode runs from the formation of Earth and the first cells, through early sea life, amphibians and a parade of dinosaurs, to the rise of mammals and primates. It then follows a line of apes and early humans (Proconsul, Ramapithecus, the australopithecines, Homo habilis) to Homo erectus mastering fire about 700,000 years ago.

## Outdated (since 1978)
- **Quote:** "With the Cambrian period came various new forms of life, medusas, phallobites, ostracoderm, the very first fish."
  **Today:** "Phallobites" is a speech-recognition error for "trilobites". In 1978 armoured jawless ostracoderms from the Ordovician were the oldest well-known fish. Since 1999, fossils from Chengjiang in China (*Myllokunmingia*, *Haikouichthys*; Shu et al., *Nature* 1999) have pushed the first fish-like vertebrates back to the early Cambrian, about 518 million years ago. Those animals were soft-bodied and were not ostracoderms.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "And here's the all-round champ, the largest beast ever to have walked the Earth. Brachiosaurus, 24 meters long, 12 high."
  **Today:** Several titanosaurs found later were bigger than *Brachiosaurus*: *Argentinosaurus* (1993), *Dreadnoughtus* (2014), *Patagotitan* (2017). The famous mounted Berlin skeleton was moved to its own genus, *Giraffatitan* (Taylor 2009). Newer mass estimates for brachiosaurs also tend to be below 50 tonnes, often around 30–45 t.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "The duck model, Anatosaurus."
  **Today:** *Anatosaurus* is no longer a valid name. Its species were moved to *Edmontosaurus* (*E. annectens*) and other genera in later revisions (Brett-Surman 1979–1990). The duck-billed hadrosaur shown is real; only the name is out of date.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "The King Tyrannosaurus, 15 by 6 meters, 8 tons"
  **Today:** "15 by 6 meters" reflects the old upright, tail-dragging "kangaroo" pose. *T. rex* is now reconstructed with a horizontal back and tail, about 12–13 m long (the largest specimen, "Sue", found in 1990, is about 12.3 m) and about 4 m tall at the hips. The 8-tonne weight is still about right.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Unlike the mastodons, these rodents could adapt to climatic conditions and natural upheavals. The old of the secondary period gradually faded away."
  **Today:** The episode suggests the dinosaurs faded out gradually because they could not cope with climate change. The end of the non-bird dinosaurs is now tied mainly to a sudden asteroid impact 66 million years ago. This was proposed by Alvarez et al. in 1980, the Chicxulub crater was identified in 1991, and a broad consensus was reached by 2010 (Schulte et al., *Science*). Deccan volcanism may also have played a part. (Two more notes: "mastodons" is probably a mistranslation of French *mastodontes*, meaning "giant beasts", since real mastodons lived much later. The early mammals were not rodents.)
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "When food became scarce, they had to leave the forest for the first time and set out into the open country. Easier from an upright position. As he straightened up, Proconsul's pelvis became stronger"
  **Today:** This is the "savanna hypothesis", in which apes became upright by moving from forest into open grassland, with *Proconsul* on the direct line. *Proconsul* is now seen as an early four-legged tree-dwelling ape, not a forerunner of upright walking. Later fossils of the earliest hominins (*Orrorin* 2001, *Sahelanthropus* 2002, *Ardipithecus* fully published 2009) show that upright walking began in wooded settings, not open savanna.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Ramapithecus, 1 meter 30."
  **Today:** In 1978 *Ramapithecus* was widely presented as the earliest human ancestor. A more complete face of the related *Sivapithecus*, found in 1980 and published by Pilbeam in 1982, together with molecular dating, showed that it was an ancestor or relative of orangutans. It has since been merged into *Sivapithecus*.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "700,000 years before our time, Pithecanthropus, or Ape Man, more correctly called Homo erectus"
  **Today:** Homo erectus appeared much earlier, about 1.9–2 million years ago in Africa (for example the Drimolen skull DNH 134, published in 2020). It had reached Georgia (Dmanisi, excavated from 1991) and Java well over a million years ago. The date of 700,000 years suits only late Asian populations such as Peking Man.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "The fabled dragon, Pteranodon, the largest flying reptile with a wingspan of 8 meters."
  **Today:** The largest known flying reptiles are azhdarchids such as *Quetzalcoatlus*, with wingspans of about 10–11 m. *Pteranodon*'s wingspan is now put at about 6–7 m at most.
  **Why it was believed:** For decades *Pteranodon* was the textbook "biggest flier". *Quetzalcoatlus* was announced to specialists only in 1975 (Lawson, *Science*) and took years to reach popular books.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Obustus was hunted to extinction. Homo habilis now reigned alone."
  **Today:** "Obustus" is a mishearing of "Robustus". No evidence shows that *Homo* hunted *Paranthropus robustus* to extinction. *Paranthropus* lived alongside *Homo* (including *H. erectus*) until about 1.2 million years ago, so *H. habilis* never "reigned alone".
  **Why it was believed:** The "killer ape" picture of early humans was everywhere in popular books (Raymond Dart; Robert Ardrey's *African Genesis*, 1961), even though specialists treated it only as speculation.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1978)
- **Quote:** "The secondary period saw the triumph of the dinosaurs, whose descendants include crocodiles, as well as birds."
  **Today:** Birds are descendants of dinosaurs. Crocodiles are not: they are cousins of the dinosaurs on a separate archosaur branch, and that branch is as old as the dinosaurs. This was established long before 1978.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "Thousands of years went by, and the great anthropomorphic apes evolved."
  **Today:** The step from *Proconsul* (about 20 million years ago) to the australopithecines (about 4–2 million years ago) took millions of years, not thousands. The error may come from the translation.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- Earth's age as "nearly 5 billion years" (it is 4.54 billion) and "more than a billion years" before the first cell: fine as round numbers. Note on scale: the dub's "billion" is the short-scale billion (10^9), since it equates 4 billion years with "40 million centuries". So these figures are not long-scale (10^12) errors.
- Wegener's continental drift: one landmass (Pangaea) that later broke apart. Basically right.
- Stegosaurus at 8 m and about 6 t with a tiny brain; dinosaurs ruling for more than 100 million years: fine.
- "Brontosaurus": the name was out of favour in 1978 but has been accepted again as a valid genus since 2015 (Tschopp et al.).
- Diplodocus called "the tallest" is probably a translation slip for "the longest". At 30 m its length is a generous estimate.
- Elasmosaurus and the Loch Ness monster are offered only as a question, not as a claim.
- The chain from Aegyptopithecus and Pliopithecus to Proconsul and the australopithecines is shown as a neat single line. It is really a branching bush, but this is acceptable for children.
- Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania (misheard as "Old Duvet"), an australopithecine brain size of about 450 cm³, and a robustus diet based mostly on plants: fine.
- Ice-age sea levels about 100 m lower than today (about 120 m at the peak), with land bridges: fine.
- Homo erectus using fire about 700,000 years ago fits current evidence (Gesher Benot Ya'aqov, about 790,000 years ago).
