# Once Upon a Time... Man — s01e02: Neanderthal Man
The episode follows a Neanderthal-era tribe as it makes stone tools (the two-sided hand axe and the cleaver), learns to cook meat over fire, quarrels over hunting grounds with a neighbouring tribe, and marks the days with a notched lunar "calendar". As the ice age returns, the tribe moves south and buries its elder, Maestro. The narrator closes by saying Neanderthals vanished "without a trace" 25,000 years ago.

## Outdated (since 1978)
- **Quote:** "25,000 years before our time, Neanderthal man ended his 100,000 years as master of Europe by disappearing without a trace."
  **Today:** Neanderthals did not vanish without a trace. Most people living today whose ancestry is from outside Africa carry about 1–2% Neanderthal DNA, which shows the two groups interbred (Green et al., *Science* 2010, Neanderthal genome).
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "25,000 years before our time, Neanderthal man ended his 100,000 years as master of Europe"
  **Today:** Improved radiocarbon dating puts the disappearance of Neanderthals at about 40,000 years ago (Higham et al., *Nature* 2014). Even 1970s handbooks usually gave about 35,000 years. The Neanderthal line was also in Europe far longer than 100,000 years: the Sima de los Huesos fossils from about 430,000 years ago are early Neanderthals (Meyer et al., *Nature* 2016).
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Was he killed by the intense cold, genetic mutation? To this day, these questions remain unanswered."
  **Today:** There is still no single agreed cause. Current research points to a mix of factors: very small, inbred populations (shown by genomics, e.g. Prüfer et al. 2014), competition with incoming *Homo sapiens*, and absorption into sapiens groups through interbreeding. Sudden climate swings may also have played a part. "Unanswered" is still partly true, but the answer is now framed very differently.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1978)
- **Quote:** "The bifacial or two-sided cutting tool, useful for a variety of tasks. The hand axe, which would become the all-purpose tool."
  **Today:** The episode shows the hand axe being invented in the Neanderthal era. Two-sided (Acheulean) hand axes were in fact made by Homo erectus more than a million years earlier: about 1.5 million years ago at Olduvai, as was already known in 1978, and now about 1.76 million years ago at Kokiselei in Kenya (2011). Neanderthals mostly made Mousterian flake tools, though they also made some hand axes.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** major

## Simplified but fine
- Pithecanthropus gradually giving way to later forms ("Sinanthropus", Swanscombe, Montmaurin, Neanderthal, Solo and Rhodesian Man) is a simplified, ladder-like family tree. "Florenscomb" and "Montmoren" are mishearings of Swanscombe and Montmaurin.
- The Neanderthal ancestors who invented cooking with fire: fire use is much older, but cooking by early humans is plausible.
- Neanderthals burying their dead fits current evidence, for example Shanidar Z (2020).
- Few people lived to old age, and violent or accidental death was common: fine.
- Neanderthals coping with ice-age cold and moving with the herds: fine.
