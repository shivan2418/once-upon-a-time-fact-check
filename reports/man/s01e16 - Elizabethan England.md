# Once Upon a Time... Man — s01e16: Elizabethan England
The episode moves quickly from Henry VIII's executions to Elizabeth I's accession and her rivalry with Philip II's Spain. Most of it follows Francis Drake's voyage around the world (1577–80) in detail. It ends with Cádiz, the Armada, Shakespeare, Elizabeth's death and the Mayflower.

## Outdated (since 1978)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Enemy warships are just outside the port as thick as flies. / ...Let me finish my game."
  **Today:** The story that Drake insisted on finishing his game of bowls when the Armada was sighted is first recorded decades later, in a 1624 pamphlet, and was expanded in the 18th century. Historians treat it as a legend. (Low tide may in fact have kept the fleet in harbour.)
  **Why it was believed:** It became one of the best-loved stories in English patriotic schoolbooks.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "The defeat of the Armada tolled the death knell for Spain's great aspirations." / "Glorious Spain had finally bowed to Elizabeth's England."
  **Today:** Spain remained Europe's leading power for decades after 1588. It rebuilt its navy in the 1590s, protected its treasure fleets, sent more armadas (1596, 1597), and saw England's own counter-armada fail in 1589. The war ended in 1604 without a clear winner.
  **Why it was believed:** English and Protestant national myth made 1588 into a turning point. Garrett Mattingly (1959) had already corrected this, and Parker and Martin (1988) did so in more detail, but popular histories lagged behind.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "On September 6, 1620, 78 men and 24 women left Plymouth on board the Mayflower. They were to become the first white Americans."
  **Today:** The English had settled permanently at Jamestown, Virginia, in 1607, and the Spanish at St. Augustine, Florida, in 1565. Spanish and French colonists had been in the Americas since 1492. (The total of 102 passengers is right; the split into men and women seems to count boys and girls with them.)
  **Why it was believed:** American and British popular history long treated the Pilgrims as the founders of America.
  **Confidence:** high

## Wrong (even in 1978)
- **Quote:** "Heaven. All that gold... Overboard! The gold overboard!"
  **Today:** When the Golden Hind ran onto a reef off Sulawesi in January 1580, the crew threw overboard about three tons of cloves, eight guns and some provisions, not the treasure. (This is a dramatised scene, but it shows a fact wrongly.)
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "At Elizabeth's death in 1603, James II, son of the beheaded Mary Stuart, acceded to the throne."
  **Today:** It was **James I** of England (James VI of Scotland). James II reigned from 1685 to 1688.
  **Confidence:** high

## Simplified but fine
- Thomas More executed in 1535; Anne Boleyn's downfall blamed on not giving Henry a son (the official charges were adultery and treason).
- Mary I married to Philip II and dying in 1558, the same year as Charles V; Elizabeth crowned at 25; the Pope excommunicating her in 1570.
- "Elizabeth's 39 articles founded the Anglican religion in 1563": the Church of England broke with Rome in 1534 and the Elizabethan Settlement came in 1559. The Articles (1563/1571) defined its doctrine.
- Drake's voyage details are accurate: leaving Plymouth in December 1577, Nuno da Silva, Doughty's trial and execution, the Pelican renamed Golden Hind, the storm south of Tierra del Fuego, Valparaíso, Callao (13 February 1579), the Cacafuego, Nova Albion (17 June 1579), Ternate, and the return on 26 September 1580 with "two years, nine months and 13 days" (correct). The northernmost point "near Vancouver Island" is disputed.
- Drake's raid on Cádiz (1587), Mary Stuart executed in 1587, the English sack of Cádiz in 1596, Drake's death on a Caribbean expedition in 1596, Philip II's death in 1598, Champlain founding Quebec in 1608.
