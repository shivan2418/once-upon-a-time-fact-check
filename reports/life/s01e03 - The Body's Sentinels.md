# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e03: The Body's Sentinels

Young white blood cells go to "school" in the thymus. There they learn to tell enemies (viruses and bacteria) from friends (red cells, platelets, helpful gut bacteria), and to check the HLA "identity card" that marks the body's own cells. The episode introduces B cells that release antibodies, killer T cells, phagocytes, macrophages, basophils and interferon. It ends with a T-cell patrol fighting off a virus in the lung.

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "These are bacteria. ... They cause chickenpox, diphtheria, meningitis, and many other illnesses."
  **Correct today:** A virus (varicella-zoster) causes chickenpox, not a bacterium. This was known well before 1987. Diphtheria is bacterial. Meningitis can be caused by either bacteria or viruses.
  **Confidence:** High

- **Quote:** "Thymus, the school for white cells."
  **Correct today:** Only T lymphocytes are trained in the thymus. B cells mature in the bone marrow, and neutrophils (the "polynuclears") and macrophages never go to the thymus. The episode shows all these cell types as pupils in the same school.
  **Confidence:** Medium

- **Quote:** "Those are basophiles. They fight allergies."
  **Correct today:** Basophils cause allergic symptoms rather than fight them. They release histamine, which drives allergic reactions.
  **Confidence:** Medium

- **Quote:** "every day 15,000 million white corpuscles come into the world"
  **Correct today:** The bone marrow makes about 50 to 100 billion neutrophils a day, plus the other white cells. That is several times more than 15 billion, and estimates from the 1970s already showed this.
  **Confidence:** Medium

## Simplified but fine

- "We drop 2,000 a second" (antibodies per cell): this is the classic textbook figure for a plasma cell, and it is still quoted.
- Each antibody fits only one kind of enemy, and the cells that make it multiply into clones when needed: correct.
- Smallpox wiped out by vaccination: correct (eradication declared in 1980).
- Helpful gut bacteria that aid digestion and make vitamins: correct.
- Interferon as a fast messenger warning neighboring cells about a virus: correct.
- "all the cells of the human body have an identical HLA mark. No other cell in the world has the same": an exaggeration, since identical twins share HLA and siblings can match (episode 4 relies on this). Red blood cells having no HLA because they lack a nucleus: essentially correct.
- Plasma making up "half the blood": it is about 55%. Fine.
- Immune cells destroying cells that turn cancerous ("transforming cells"): this idea, called immune surveillance, is still accepted.
- The route from the jugular vein to the superior vena cava, right atrium ("oracle" is Whisper for "auricle") and lung: correct.
