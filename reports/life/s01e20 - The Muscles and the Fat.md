# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e20: The Muscles and the Fat

The episode explains how nerves tell muscles to contract, and how muscles burn sugar with oxygen for energy, switching to fat reserves when the sugar runs out. It contrasts fit Peter with his overweight friend Jumbo, whose extra fat strains his spine, heart and lungs. It ends with a doctor ordering exercise and a diet.

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "The muscle, the most important system in the human body, making up more than half its weight."
  **Correct today:** Skeletal muscle makes up about 30–40% of body weight in adults (roughly 40% in a fit young man, less in women and older people), not more than half. This was already the textbook figure in the 1980s.
  **Confidence:** High
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- "Six oxygen bubbles. That should make, uh, 685 kilocalories": this is right. Burning one molecule of glucose uses six of oxygen, and a mole of glucose releases about 686 kcal.
- "Five hundred muscles": the usual count is about 600 or more, depending on how you count. Close enough.
- Platelets sent to deal with "stubborn lipids" to "ease the circulation": platelets don't clear fat. This reads as a cartoon gag, not a real claim.
- The doctor's "only grilled meats and salads" diet for an overweight child: today's advice would be a balanced, family-based approach rather than a restrictive diet. It's a passing line, though, not a medical claim.
- Heart rate rising to around 200 while sprinting, and the body switching to fat once sugar runs low: broadly correct.
