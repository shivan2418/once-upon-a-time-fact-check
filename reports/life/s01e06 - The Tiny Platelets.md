# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e06: The Tiny Platelets

Young platelets learn their job of patching damaged blood vessel walls, and fibrin "ropes" (made from fibrinogen by thrombin) bind them into clots. An old platelet tells the story of a disease in which antibodies attacked the body's own platelets. When Peter cuts his thumb with a dirty knife, platelets and clotting factors stop the bleeding while white cells fight a staphylococcus infection, and the pus is shown to be dead white cells.

## Outdated (since 1987)

- **Quote:** "He injected the bloodstream with Trojan horse platelets. They were full of medicine that killed any antibody that touched them."
  **Correct today:** The disease is immune thrombocytopenia (ITP). The experimental "Trojan horse" treatment (platelets loaded with the drug vinblastine, 1970s to 80s) was aimed at the macrophages that eat antibody-coated platelets, not at the antibodies, and it never became standard. Today ITP is treated with steroids, immunoglobulin infusions (IVIG), rituximab, and drugs that boost platelet production (romiplostim and eltrombopag, approved in 2008).
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "This is the bone marrow, where nearly half a billion blood platelets are produced every day." (repeated at the end: "Your body produces nearly half a billion platelets every day")
  **Correct today:** The dub most likely uses the older British long-scale "billion" (a million million, like the French *billion*), so "half a billion" means about 500,000 million, the same figure episode 4 gives as "500 billion." That is still about 3 to 5 times too high: the body makes about 100 to 150 thousand million platelets a day, a rate already measured with isotope-labelled platelets in the late 1960s (Harker and Finch, 1969). Platelets live 7 to 10 days and there are about a million million in circulation.
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

- **Quote:** "I'm calling in the Prostaglandin squad immediately. ... Only Prostaglandin can remove the blockage."
  **Correct today:** Clots are dissolved by the fibrinolytic system: plasmin, activated by tPA (approved as a clot-busting drug in 1987). Some prostaglandins, such as prostacyclin, stop platelets from clumping, while others, such as thromboxane, make them clump more. None of them dissolves an existing blockage.
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- "We lived for only ten days": correct (7 to 10 days).
- Wall damage caused by "harmful fats, acid, or even adrenaline": broadly in line with how blood vessel linings are thought to be damaged.
- Thrombin acting on fibrinogen to make fibrin: correct.
- Antibodies attacking the body's own platelets: a correct description of ITP.
- Pus as dead white cells that fought germs: correct.
- "platelets shrink with age": still debated (platelet size mostly reflects how they were produced, but some studies show older platelets lose content). Too uncertain to count.
- Platelets staying on the wall "Forever, naturally": unclear in context, so not counted. Clots are actually remodeled and cleared.
