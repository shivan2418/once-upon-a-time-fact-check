# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e12: The Ear

Sound waves vibrate the eardrum. The three small bones (hammer, anvil, stirrup) pass the vibration to the cochlea, where hair cells turn it into nerve signals, and the brain turns those signals into sound. The episode also covers an ear infection that spreads up the Eustachian tube, noise damage that cannot be undone, and the semicircular canals that help with balance.

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "polarize the cell, let the sodium ion messengers in" (hair cells of the cochlea)
  **Today:** The inner-ear hair cells are activated mainly by **potassium** (plus some calcium) flowing in from the potassium-rich fluid around them, not by sodium. The unusual potassium-rich fluid had been known since the 1950s, and the potassium-based mechanism was well described by the early 1980s. (Sodium is right for ordinary nerve impulses further along.)
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- "amplify the vibrations two times": the middle ear actually raises sound pressure about 20-fold, but the transcript may have misheard "twenty". Not counted as an error.
- Staphylococcus as the cause of a childhood middle-ear infection: the usual germs are pneumococcus and *Haemophilus*, but the episode only shows staph as the invader and doesn't claim it is the usual cause.
- Head tilt detected by the "semicircular canals": the canals sense turning, and steady tilt is sensed by other parts of the inner ear (the otolith organs). A simplification.
- "Without our ears, we could never stand up": an exaggeration. People who lose inner-ear balance can learn to rely on their eyes and body sense.
- "in those days [cavemen], our hearing was much more acute": speculation, not a checkable medical claim.
- Noise-induced hearing loss is permanent: still true in humans.
- 140 dB is painful: correct.
