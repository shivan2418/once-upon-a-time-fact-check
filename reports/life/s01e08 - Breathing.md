# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e08: Breathing

The episode introduces the alveoli, where blood gives off carbon dioxide and picks up oxygen. A gang of bacteria and viruses tries to get in through the mouth. The defenders in the tonsils wipe out most of them, and the survivors try the lungs, where infected cells send interferon alarms and order antibodies. It also shows dust and pollution building up in the lungs.

## Common belief at the time

- **Quote:** "That's where oxygen is transformed into carbon dioxide."
  **Correct today:** The oxygen we breathe ends up as water. The carbon dioxide we breathe out is made from the carbon in food (sugars and fats), which is broken down in the mitochondria of cells all over the body. This was shown with isotope tracer experiments decades before 1987. (The line also seems to place this "real breathing" in the lungs, which is wrong too, though the transcript is unclear there.)
  **Why it was believed:** School textbooks summed up respiration as "glucose + oxygen gives carbon dioxide + water" and "we breathe in oxygen and breathe out carbon dioxide," which reads naturally as oxygen turning into CO2; that inhaled oxygen ends up in water was known mainly from isotope tracer studies.
  **Confidence:** High

## Simplified but fine

- "300 million, 600 or 800 million? Opinions differ." (number of alveoli): the modern estimate is about 480 million (Ochs, 2004). The episode admits the uncertainty, so this is not an error.
- Alveolar surface of "70 to 90 square meters": within the range of current estimates.
- The nose filtering and warming air better than the mouth: correct.
- Tonsils acting as a front-line immune checkpoint: correct.
- Interferon alarms between cells, and the call to produce antibodies after a virus is identified: correct.
- Dust and pollution accumulating in the lungs: correct.
