# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e18: The Lymphatic System

Lymph is a clear fluid carried in its own vessels. It passes through lymph nodes, which act as checkpoints where immune cells catch germs, and drains into the blood through the thoracic duct, carrying fats absorbed from the gut. An old red blood cell (Professor Globus) dies in the spleen after 120 days. A staph infection from a cat scratch, after a bout of flu, swells a lymph node and needs an antibiotic.

## Common belief at the time

- **Quote:** "[Lymphocytes] originate here in the lymphatic system, which accounts for the derivation of their name."
  **Today:** Lymphocytes are made in the bone marrow, and T cells then mature in the thymus. They multiply in lymph nodes and are named after lymph, but that is not where they start. This was known by the 1960s–70s. (The transcript is incomplete here, so the exact wording is uncertain.)
  **Why it was believed:** Lymphocytes multiply in lymph nodes and are named after lymph, and older textbooks and encyclopedias described the lymph nodes as where lymphocytes are made; the bone-marrow and thymus origin, established in the 1960s, reached popular references slowly.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "The spleen stores the metal and uses it to create new red corpuscles."
  **Today:** The spleen breaks down old red blood cells and recycles their iron, but in adults new red blood cells are made in the bone marrow, not the spleen. The spleen only makes red blood cells before birth.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine

- "all the other components of blood are found in lymph": lymph has much less protein and few platelets, but it is broadly blood plasma without the red cells.
- "there are no red corpuscles because there's no oxygen in lymph": the reasoning is backwards (red cells are too big to leak out of blood vessels), but it is a passing line.
- Calling a lymph node a "ganglion, or gland": translated from the French term. "Swollen glands" is everyday English for this.
- A staph infection from a cat scratch: possible. The classic "cat scratch disease" is caused by *Bartonella* (identified around 1992), but the episode doesn't claim to be showing that disease.
- "The antibiotic will give a big boost to his natural defenses": antibiotics kill bacteria directly rather than boosting the immune system, but the overall message is fine.
- 120-day red cell lifespan and "two million new red corpuscles ... every second": correct.
- Flu weakening defences, leading to a bacterial infection: correct.
