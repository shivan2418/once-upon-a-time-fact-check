# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e02: Birth

Hundreds of millions of sperm set out, and only a few reach the egg. The fertilized egg divides, implants in the womb, and becomes an embryo fed by the placenta. Newly made cells are sent off by type (bone, liver, muscle, eye and so on) to build the body, and the episode tracks the fetus's growth up to a 50 cm, 3.2 kg baby at nine months.

## Outdated (since 1987)

- **Quote:** "There are 200 different kinds of you"
  **Correct today:** "About 200 cell types" was the standard textbook number. Since the 2010s, single-cell studies such as the Human Cell Atlas have found several hundred main types and thousands of subtypes. The real number depends on how finely you split them.
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- Only a small share of sperm get past the cervix, many die on the way, and few reach the egg: correct.
- "The neck of the womb closes up again to preserve the fertilized egg": the cervix does become sealed by a thicker mucus plug, driven by the hormone progesterone. The timing is compressed, but this is not an explicit falsehood.
- Implantation within a few days, and the placenta forming from the outer membrane: correct.
- Embryo sizes (6 mm at day 35, 12 mm at day 42, 20 mm at day 50): roughly right. Later growth figures in the transcript are garbled by Whisper.
- "Collagen and ovalbumin right away": ovalbumin is egg-white protein, which humans do not make. This is probably a dubbing slip for "albumin," so it is not counted as an error.
- "I need a U": RNA does use U (uracil) in place of DNA's T. Correct.
- 50 cm and 3.2 kg at birth: typical.
