# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e14: The Mouth and the Teeth

The mouth is the first stage of digestion. Teeth cut, tear and grind, saliva starts digestion and helps keep the mouth clean, and the tongue detects tastes. Sugar-loving bacteria (*Streptococcus mutans*) turn sugar into acid, dig a cavity through the enamel and threaten to reach the bloodstream until a dentist steps in. The message is to brush your teeth and cut down on sugar.

## Outdated (since 1987)

- **Quote:** "the tongue is sensitive to four basic elements of taste: bitter, acidic, sweet and salty"
  **Today:** There is a widely accepted fifth basic taste, **umami** (savoury, the taste of glutamate). It was proposed in Japan in 1908 but only became accepted in the West after its taste receptors were found in 2000–2002.
  **Confidence:** high

## Simplified but fine

- "The mouth produces two liters of saliva per day": modern estimates are about 0.5–1.5 litres, so a bit high, but within the loose figures in older textbooks.
- Taste messages having to "arrive together" so the brain can combine all four tastes: a story device, not a scientific claim.
- "their only source of nourishment is sugar": decay bacteria also feed on starch and other carbohydrates, but sugar is the main culprit.
- Brushing after every sugary snack: today's advice puts more weight on brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste and eating sugar less often, but the episode's advice isn't harmful.
- "tooth enamel is the hardest material in the human body": correct.
- Mouth germs can spread through the blood: correct.
