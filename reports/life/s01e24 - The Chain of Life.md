# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e24: The Chain of Life

The episode traces food chains back to the sun and shows plants using sunlight to make sugar. A prehistoric man with no vitamin C develops scurvy. It ends by warning that modern people eat too much rich food and exercise too little, while an old food-grabbing instinct in the brain overrides the signal that we're full.

## Outdated (since 1987)

- **Quote:** "Order from the primitive brain. Nourishment in sight. To be seized." / "Memories and ancient instincts still exist, which the modern brain can't always subdue"
  **Correct today:** This is the "triune brain" model, which pictured a "primitive" (reptile-like) brain buried under a newer, rational brain. Brain scientists have since abandoned it. Appetite is controlled by connected circuits in the hypothalamus and reward system, steered by hormones such as leptin (discovered in 1994) and ghrelin (1999). The fact that people overeat is real, but the "two brains fighting" explanation is outdated.
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- "It's the sun's heat that develops everything which is nourishing": strictly, plants use the sun's light, not its heat. The very next scenes say "sunlight" and show light particles (photons), so this is just loose wording.
- Scurvy is caused by lack of vitamin C, the body can't make vitamin C, and scurvy causes bleeding from fragile blood vessels: correct. Listing "fever" as a symptom is a stretch, but minor.
- "We eat ... about 70,000 times" in a lifetime: a reasonable estimate.
- The warning about too much food and too little exercise: still current advice.
