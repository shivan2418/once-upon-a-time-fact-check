# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e09: The Brain

The episode presents the brain as three evolutionary layers: a reptilian brain, a primitive mammalian brain and a modern neocortex. It explains the brain's large need for oxygen and sugar, how neurons pass messages through synapses, and how the medulla, hypothalamus and cerebellum handle automatic jobs such as breathing, thirst and balance. It also explains memory as storing sensory information (a rose's color and thorns), and the body map in the brain where more sensitive parts get larger areas.

## Outdated (since 1987)

- **Quote:** "The reptilian brain, the paleocortex or primitive mammalian brain, and the neocortex or modern man's brain. Unfortunately, communication between them is not always perfect." (and "the most primitive part always tries to dominate")
  **Correct today:** The "triune brain" model has been abandoned by neuroscientists. Brains did not evolve by stacking new layers on top of old ones, and there is no separate "reptile brain" fighting the "thinking brain" (see for example Cesario et al., 2020).
  **Confidence:** High
  **Severity:** major

- **Quote:** "Others say it probably has more than a hundred billion." / "Brain contains a hundred thousand million cells."
  **Correct today:** The human brain has about 86 billion neurons (Azevedo and Herculano-Houzel, 2009), plus a similar number of support cells (glia). The old "100 billion neurons" figure was an estimate that had never been measured. The episode also says "the brain has 10 billion nerve cells," which is too low.
  **Confidence:** High
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "Approximately a hundred thousand years ago, the neocortex developed."
  **Correct today:** All mammals have a neocortex, so it is well over 100 million years old. Only its large expansion in humans is recent, and even that took millions of years.
  **Confidence:** High
  **Severity:** major

- **Quote:** "Nerve cell has 100,000 synaptic connections."
  **Correct today:** A typical neuron has a few thousand to about 10,000 synapses. Only special cells, such as the Purkinje cells of the cerebellum, reach about 100,000 or more.
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

- **Quote:** "It uses 2,000 liters of blood a day ... This is 20% of the body's oxygen requirements."
  **Correct today:** The 20% share of the body's oxygen is right. But blood flow to the brain is about 750 ml a minute, which is about 1,000 to 1,100 liters a day, so 2,000 is roughly double.
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- A few minutes without oxygen causing permanent brain damage: correct.
- "Neurons are too sensitive to come into direct contact with us" (the blood-brain barrier): correct.
- The brain also needing sugar: correct.
- The medulla oblongata controlling breathing and heart rate, the hypothalamus controlling thirst and temperature, the cerebellum controlling balance, and spinal reflexes: correct.
- Making new proteins being needed to store long-term memories: still accepted. The picture of one protein per fact is a cartoon.
- More sensitive body parts (face, hands) having larger brain areas: correct (this map is called the homunculus).
- "Represents 85% of the cerebral mass": close to the modern figure of about 80% of brain mass for the cortex.
- "The human brain developed approximately three billion years ago": probably a garbled reference to the start of life's evolutionary history, so not counted.
- "Little chemical messengers now arrive at the nucleus of the neuron": probably means the cell body. Not counted.
