# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e05: The Blood

The episode follows red blood cells carrying oxygen and carbon dioxide through the blood vessels, with white "police" cells clearing out bacteria such as streptococci. It covers how long red cells live, how they are replaced, how cells release stored sugar during a shortage, and an antibody-led fight against a flu virus.

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "a signal goes out to the part of the cell where sugar is stored. The Golgai bodies. When the signal is received, sugar is released through the walls of the cell."
  **Correct today:** Cells store sugar as glycogen, in small grains in the cell's fluid (cytoplasm), not in the Golgi. Only the liver (and to a small extent the kidney) can release that sugar back into the blood. Muscle keeps its glycogen for its own use. The Golgi packages proteins.
  **Confidence:** High
  **Severity:** minor

- **Quote:** "One cubic millimeter of liquid blood plasma contains five million red corpuscles and 700,000 phagocytes, or white."
  **Correct today:** Normal blood has about 4,000 to 11,000 white cells per cubic millimeter, so 700,000 is about 100 times too high. (The red cell figure is right, though the cells are in whole blood, not plasma.) This could be a dubbing slip for 7,000, but the figure as spoken is wrong. (likely a translation/dubbing slip)
  **Confidence:** High
  **Severity:** minor

- **Quote:** "blood vessels of the human body. Their surface area is 700,000 square meters."
  **Correct today:** The inner surface of all the blood vessels (mostly capillaries) is usually estimated at a few hundred to about 1,000 square meters. 700,000 is roughly 1,000 times too large.
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- "Every day, the heart beats a hundred thousand times": correct.
- "more than a hundred thousand kilometers" of vessels: this is the standard popular figure. It is debated, but too uncertain to count as an error.
- Red cells living "100 days, 120 at the outside," then being removed in the spleen: correct.
- "Over two million of us per second" (new red cells): correct.
- The route "4,000 times a day": probably too high, since a full loop of the circulation takes about a minute. The number is unclear, so it is not counted.
- Macrophages and platelets helping clear blockages, and streptococci as routine invaders: fine as a cartoon.
