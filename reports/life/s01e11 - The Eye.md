# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e11: The Eye

Light is shown as photons that bounce off objects into the eye, where the lens focuses them onto the rods and cones of the retina. The signal travels along the optic nerve to the brain, which does the real "seeing" and interprets images (optical illusions, an orange vs. the sun). The episode also covers tears, which clean the eye and contain germ-killing enzymes (lysozyme).

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "The left brain deals with the signals from the right eye and vice versa."
  **Today:** Each side of the brain handles the opposite half of what you see, using input from *both* eyes. About half the fibres from each eye cross over at the optic chiasm. This has been known since the 1800s. A simple crossover like the one described happens in some animals, not in humans.
  **Confidence:** high

## Simplified but fine

- "300,000 kilometres per second" and "around the world seven times" in a second (really about 7.5): correct.
- Vitamin/carotene units helping the eye: correct (vitamin A is needed for night vision).
- Rods and cones named "from their shapes": correct.
- Tears with "germ-killing additives" and lysozyme "cleaning enzymes": correct. Lysozyme fights bacteria rather than the "viral conjunctivitis" mentioned, but that is a small slip in a cartoon scene.
- Rubbing eyes with dirty hands can cause conjunctivitis: still sound advice.
