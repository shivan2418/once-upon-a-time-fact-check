# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e13: The Skin

A tour down through the three layers of skin (epidermis, dermis, hypodermis), showing melanocytes, sweat glands, goosebump muscles, fat and the various touch, pressure, heat and cold sensors. A mosquito bite and a scratch infected with streptococci show how the skin's defences respond.

## Outdated (since 1987)

None found.

## Common belief at the time

- **Quote:** "It also helps the body to breathe"
  **Today:** Human skin takes in only a tiny amount of oxygen (around 1% or less of what the body needs). Skin "breathing" matters in frogs, not humans. The old idea that covering the whole body in paint suffocates you is a myth.
  **Why it was believed:** Older physiology and school texts listed "respiration" among the skin's functions, and the idea that skin must breathe was spread by the story (popularized by the 1964 film *Goldfinger*) that painting a body gold suffocates it.
  **Confidence:** medium

- **Quote:** "Ruffini, the heat sensor" (plus the cold-sensing "Krause" character)
  **Today:** Ruffini endings sense skin stretch and steady pressure, not heat. Temperature is sensed by free nerve endings. The idea that each type of corpuscle senses one temperature comes from the late 1800s and had been disproved by the 1960s–70s, though some textbooks still repeated it.
  **Why it was believed:** Von Frey's 1890s scheme giving heat to Ruffini endings and cold to Krause end bulbs was still printed in many textbooks and encyclopedias in the 1980s, long after physiologists had shown that free nerve endings do the work.
  **Confidence:** high

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "We protect the skin by turning brown to create a suntan. And we also manufacture vitamin D" (said by melanocytes)
  **Today:** Vitamin D is made mostly in keratinocytes, the main skin cells, not in the pigment cells. Melanin (the brown pigment) actually *reduces* vitamin D production. Both facts were published in the early 1980s.
  **Confidence:** medium

- **Quote:** "Antibodies, who kill them. Or histamine, which destroys them chemically."
  **Today:** Histamine does not destroy germs or poisons. It is a signal that widens blood vessels and causes swelling, redness and itching (it causes the itch of a mosquito bite).
  **Confidence:** high

- **Quote:** "The skin draws one-third of the blood circulation."
  **Today:** At rest the skin gets about 5–10% of the blood the heart pumps. It can get much more when the body is shedding heat, but one-third is not a normal figure.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine

- "every single cell in that layer is replaced within 20 days": usual estimates are about 4 to 6 weeks, but turnover figures vary widely, so this is loose rather than wrong.
- "About three million" sweat glands: correct (2–4 million).
- Sweat "gets rid of waste products": waste removal through sweat is minor (its main job is cooling), but this is a simplification.
- A suntan as protection: a tan gives only weak protection, but melanin does block UV.
- Malpighi (and the other Italians) discovering skin and blood structures about 300 years ago: roughly right historically.
- Hands and face have the most nerve endings: broadly correct.
