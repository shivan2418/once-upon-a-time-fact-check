# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e15: The Digestion

Food travels from the oesophagus to the stomach, where acid and enzymes break it down, then on to the small intestine. There bile and pancreatic enzymes finish the job and nutrients are absorbed through the villi (fats go into the lymph). In the large intestine water is taken back and friendly bacteria make vitamins (B, K) and keep harmful germs such as Salmonella in check. The episode warns about overeating and not washing your hands.

## Outdated (since 1987)

- **Quote:** "That's the appendix, dear." / "Well, what's it for?" / "Absolutely nothing apart from getting appendicitis."
  **Today:** The appendix is no longer seen as useless. It contains immune (lymphoid) tissue and may act as a "safe house" from which good gut bacteria can regrow after illness (Bollinger and colleagues, 2007). People without an appendix may be more likely to get repeat *C. difficile* infections.

## Simplified but fine

- "We're E. coli bacteria. We live in the small intestine": E. coli lives mainly in the large intestine, but this is a minor detail.
- "There are thousands of friendly bacteria": the real number is tens of trillions. Read as casual speech, not a count.
- The stomach breaking down "fiber, minerals, vitamins, starches, sugars, proteins": in fact the stomach mainly breaks down protein, and most digestion happens in the small intestine.
- Bile helps "digest fats": strictly speaking, bile breaks fat into small droplets and enzymes do the digesting.
- Salmonella blamed on unwashed hands: Salmonella usually comes from food (eggs, poultry), but hand-washing does help.
- Gut bacteria making vitamins K and B: correct.
- Fats are absorbed into the lymph: correct.
