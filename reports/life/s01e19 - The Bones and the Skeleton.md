# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e19: The Bones and the Skeleton

The episode shows how a child's soft cartilage is slowly turned into bone by osteoblasts, helped by calcium, phosphorus and vitamin D, and how osteoclasts break bone down so it can be reshaped. Peter and Claire break their legs skiing, and we watch the fractures heal while bacteria try to infect Peter's open wound. It ends with diet advice: milk, cheese and sunshine.

## Common belief at the time

- **Quote:** "does a broken bone ever regain its full strength? ... Heavens, yes. In fact, it's a great deal stronger than before at the site of the fracture"
  **Correct today:** For a while after healing, the lump of new bone around a break is thicker. Over months to years the body reshapes it back to roughly normal, so the bone ends up about as strong as before, not "a great deal stronger." The idea that a healed break is the strongest spot is a common myth.
  **Why it was believed:** The callus around a healing fracture is visibly thicker than normal bone, and the saying that a healed break becomes the strongest point of the bone was widely repeated by doctors, coaches and popular books.
  **Confidence:** Medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- "Enzymes bring urgently needed calcium to the site of the break": enzymes don't carry calcium around (the blood does), but it's a harmless cartoon shortcut.
- "Never put weight on a fracture while it's healing" and bed rest to "save energy": doctors now often let patients put some weight on a stable fracture early. For an open (compound) shinbone fracture like Peter's, though, keeping weight off at first is still normal.
- "over 200 separate bones" and the skeleton being "scarcely 20%" of body mass: close enough (about 206 bones, and roughly 15% of body weight).
- "Without the demolishers, the builders would go on till the whole body was solid bone": exaggerated, but the balance between osteoblasts and osteoclasts is real. When osteoclasts fail, the result is a disease of overly dense bone (osteopetrosis).
- Eating milk and cheese and getting sunshine for vitamin D to build bone: still reasonable advice.
