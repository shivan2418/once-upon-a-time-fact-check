# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e17: The Kidneys

The liver turns toxic ammonia into urea, which travels in the blood to the kidneys. There about a million filtering units per kidney (glomeruli and Bowman's capsules) filter the blood, and hormones (ADH, aldosterone) help reclaim about 99% of the water, salt and sugar. The waste leaves as urine through the ureters to the bladder.

## Outdated (since 1987)

- **Quote:** "the kidney had been severely overloaded by alcohol abuse. And it failed to get rid of all the waste products ... If the kidney stone stays in place..." / "As soon as I gave up drinking"
  **Today:** Alcohol is not considered a major cause of kidney stones. Large studies since the 1990s (Curhan and colleagues, 1996/1998, and later analyses) found that moderate beer and wine drinking goes with a slightly *lower* stone risk, probably because of the extra fluid. The main risk factors are not drinking enough water, diet (salt, animal protein, oxalate), genetics and metabolic conditions. Heavy drinking is still harmful in other ways.

## Simplified but fine

- The kidneys clean "one liter of blood per minute" out of the heart's 5 litres: correct (about 20–25% of the heart's output).
- "each kidney has a million" filtering units: correct.
- "99% ... is restored to the body": correct for the water in the filtered fluid.
- Ammonia is turned into urea in the liver: correct.
- Some kidney infections can colour urine red: correct.
