# Once Upon a Time... Life — s01e25: Repairs and Changes

While Peter sleeps, his brain sorts the day's memories and mixes them into dreams, and his cells carry on repairs. A copying mistake in a cell creates harmful proteins, which the body quickly cleans up. The episode explains that young bodies repair themselves fast, while old bodies wear out.

## Wrong (even in 1987)

- **Quote:** "The RNA instructions for assembling proteins are all scrambled. I want you to get some repair enzymes down there" ... "You've created wild genes, you have."
  **Correct today:** Cells don't repair damaged messenger RNA, the short-lived working copy of a gene. They break it down and make a fresh copy from the DNA. A mistake in an RNA copy can't create new or "wild" genes, because genes live in the DNA. Only changes to the DNA itself (mutations) alter genes.
  **Confidence:** Medium (cartoon framing, but the claim is explicit)
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine

- The brain sorts and files memories during sleep: this fits modern research on sleep and memory well, and is arguably ahead of its time.
- "we do dream every night ... it's just that we forget": broadly accepted.
- Repairs happening while the body sleeps: broadly fine.
- Ageing explained as cells simply "wearing out": a simplification. Ageing is now explained by a list of specific causes, such as accumulated DNA damage and shortening of the protective caps on chromosomes (telomeres). But the episode doesn't claim anything false.
- "Heparin ... has to be eliminated when it tries to stop the healing process": unclear and probably a garbled dub line, so not counted.
