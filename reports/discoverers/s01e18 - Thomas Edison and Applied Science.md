# Once Upon a Time... The Discoverers — s01e18: Thomas Edison and Applied Science
Maestro plays an early phonograph and tells Edison's life story. It starts with the curious boy in Port Huron and his home schooling, then covers his years as a newsboy and telegraph operator, the vote counter and stock-ticker sales, Menlo Park, the phonograph, the light bulb and Pearl Street, and the film studio. The lesson is that Edison was less a scientist than an inventor who turned science into everyday devices.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Now, this thread is carbonized."
  **Today:** The episode presents the incandescent lamp as Edison's alone. In England, Joseph Swan had shown carbon-filament lamps in 1878–79, and the two later merged their British interests as "Ediswan" (1883). Edison's real achievement was a complete system: a high-resistance filament, generators, wiring, meters and a central station. A Menlo Park team built it, including Charles Batchelor, Francis Upton and John Kruesi.
  **Why it was believed:** American popular histories told the light bulb as a lone-genius story, and Edison worked hard to promote that version himself.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "but it's Edison who invented it."
  **Today:** William Kennedy Laurie Dickson did most of the work on the Kinetograph camera and Kinetoscope viewer in Edison's lab (1889–92). He built on earlier work by Marey, Muybridge and Le Prince. Gordon Hendricks documented Dickson's role in *The Edison Motion Picture Myth* (1961). The show does mention the Lumières' projected cinema (1895).
  **Why it was believed:** The patents and publicity carried Edison's name, and popular books repeated "Edison invented the movies".
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1994)
- **Quote:** "When you add water, it gives off hydrogen."
  **Today:** In Edison's own story he gave his friend Michael Oates Seidlitz powders. These are sodium bicarbonate and tartaric acid, and with water they give off carbon dioxide, which is heavier than air. No powder a boy would have had gives off hydrogen in water, so the chemistry is off even though the anecdote is real.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "battle that'll decide the war general grant against general johnson"
  **Today:** This is Shiloh, Grant against Albert Sidney Johnston, fought on 6–7 April **1862**, not in 1861 as the narration says just before. It did not decide the war. Edison's trick fits the sources: he telegraphed the headlines ahead to stations and sold about 1,000 papers at higher and higher prices.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- The first tinfoil phonograph dates from late 1877 (patented 1878). Scott's phonautograph (1857) could record sound but not play it back, so Edison's machine really was the first to reproduce sound. The groove was embossed in tinfoil, not cut.
- The goose-egg sitting and the teacher who called him "addled" (here "imbecile") come from Edison family stories. His mother taught him at home.
- "Without exaggerating, the greatest inventor" is Maestro's opinion. Edison held 1,093 US patents, many of them from team work.
- He sold newspapers on the Grand Trunk Railway from about 1859 and printed the *Grand Trunk Herald* on the train at 15 (1862).
- The ice-jam message to Sarnia, whistled in Morse by locomotive, comes from early biographies and has never been confirmed.
- Milton Adams was his friend in Boston (1868). Nobel patented dynamite (nitroglycerin absorbed in kieselguhr) in 1867.
- His first patent, the electric vote recorder (1869), was rejected by Congress because legislators wanted slow votes. That matches the sources.
- He arrived broke in New York in 1869 aged 22, fixed the Laws ("Lars") gold indicator and was hired at $300 a month.
- The $40,000 from Gold and Stock/Western Union for his ticker work, when he hoped for $3,000–5,000, is Edison's own famous anecdote. The quadruplex sale was messier: Western Union (Orton) and Jay Gould fought over it in 1874–75.
- He married Mary Stilwell on 25 December 1871. The forgotten-wedding gag is a legend.
- Menlo Park (1876) is usually called the first industrial research lab, though German dye firms set up research labs around the same time.
- He demonstrated an electric railway at Menlo Park in 1880. Pearl Street (1882) was the first US central power station, a few months after Edison's own Holborn Viaduct station in London.
- Faraday's induction and motor principle is stated correctly.
- To its credit, the show says the telephone came from Bell and Elisha Gray ("Alicia Gray" is a mishearing) and that Edison improved it with the carbon transmitter.
- Spring-motor phonographs came in the 1890s. The Black Maria studio (1893) turned on a turntable to follow the sun, as shown.
- "More of an inventor than a scientist" is a fair verdict, though he did observe the "Edison effect" (1883).
