# Once Upon a Time... The Discoverers — s01e07: Leonardo da Vinci, a Jack-of-All-Trades
The episode follows Leonardo from his training under Verrocchio in Florence to Ludovico Sforza's Milan. It shows his famous letter offering military engineering, the giant bronze horse that was never cast, the ideal city drawn after the plague, the Last Supper, and the French invasion that ended Ludovico's rule. A fast montage then covers Isabella d'Este, Cesare Borgia, the Mona Lisa and his death in France in 1519. The lesson is Leonardo as the universal mind who was too far ahead of his time and too curious to finish things.

## Outdated (since 1994)
- **Quote:** "Salai, what have you done with my drawings for the bicycle?"
  **Today:** The "Leonardo bicycle" sketch in the Codex Atlanticus was first reported in 1974, after the codex was restored. In 1994 it was still widely accepted, with Augusto Marinoni attributing it to a pupil (Salaì) copying a lost Leonardo design. That is clearly where the episode's Salai joke comes from. In 1997–98 Hans-Erhard Lessing and others showed that the sketch was a modern addition made after the restoration, and most historians now reject it. Leonardo did draw chain drives, but never a bicycle.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "taken as an apprentice in the workshop of a great artist by the talent of his young apprentice he"
  **Today:** Vasari's story is that Verrocchio "never painted again" after seeing Leonardo's angel in the *Baptism of Christ*. Art historians treat it as a legend: Verrocchio's workshop went on producing paintings, and Verrocchio had always been mainly a sculptor. The painted-shield (rotella) story told just before this also comes only from Vasari.
  **Why it was believed:** Vasari's *Lives* (1550/1568) was for centuries the main source for Leonardo's youth, and its anecdotes passed straight into popular biography.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1994)
- **Quote:** "He sent it to his brother-in-law, Deste, to cast cannons."
  **Today:** In November 1494 Ludovico sent the roughly 70 tonnes of bronze set aside for the horse to Ercole d'Este of Ferrara to be made into cannon. Ercole was Ludovico's **father-in-law** (Ludovico had married Ercole's daughter Beatrice), not his brother-in-law. Ercole's son Alfonso was the brother-in-law.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- Lorenzo de' Medici, Ludovico Sforza ("il Moro") acting as ruler for his nephew Gian Galeazzo, and Leonardo moving to Milan in about 1482 are right. Gian Galeazzo died in 1494 in suspicious circumstances, and Ludovico invited Charles VIII into Italy.
- The letter to Ludovico does offer portable bridges, siege machines, mortars "like a hailstorm", armoured cars and catapults, with painting and sculpture mentioned last. The water-walking shoes and diving gear come from his notebooks, not the letter, which may never have been sent.
- The *Ginevra de' Benci* portrait, the Pazzi conspiracy (1478, Giuliano killed), Verrocchio's Colleoni statue in Venice and Leonardo entering Verrocchio's workshop as a teenager are right.
- The Sforza horse took about 16 years and about 70–80 tonnes of bronze, and only a clay model was ever made. "Celia Gallagher" is Cecilia Gallerani (*Lady with an Ermine*).
- The plague of 1484–85 hit Milan hard ("half the population" is high). The ideal-city plan really did have 10 towns of 30,000 people, streets on two levels and drains.
- Salaì ("little devil") joined him in 1490. Leonardo visited Pavia in 1490 to advise on the cathedral and admired Dondi's astrarium clock.
- Savonarola's rise in the 1490s, his excommunication by Alexander VI (1497), the failed trial by fire and his execution (1498) are right.
- The needle-grinding machine and Leonardo's estimate of 60,000 ducats a year are in his notebooks. Leonardo did not invent most of the machines in the closing list. Many came from earlier Sienese engineers such as Taccola and Francesco di Giorgio, although the episode only says he "worked on" them.
- The Last Supper was painted for Santa Maria delle Grazie in 1495–98 in an experimental tempera/oil technique on dry plaster, which is why it decayed. The story of the prior and Judas's face is from Vasari.
- Ludovico died a prisoner in France (Loches, 1508). The list of Isabella d'Este, the Venetian Turkish plans, Cesare Borgia (1502), the *Battle of Anghiari* failure, the Mona Lisa (begun in 1503), Melzi, the Turin "self-portrait" (disputed), Francis I in 1516 and his death at Amboise in 1519 is accurate.
- Freud (1910) did write that Leonardo was "like a man who awoke too early in the darkness, while the others were all still asleep."
