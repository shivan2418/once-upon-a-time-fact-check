# Once Upon a Time... The Discoverers — s01e26: Tomorrow
The final episode imagines 2020. There are robot butlers, suborbital airliners, school bags with built-in computers linked to a "memory bank", and a World Assembly that votes on colonising Mars, with the arguments over cost, health, radiation and robots versus humans. A rogue asteroid forces the Mars ship to be diverted. The episode ends on terraforming Mars and future starships (nuclear, laser-sail, fusion, antimatter), arguing that discovery is part of human nature.

## Outdated (since 1994)
- **Quote:** "and rising to a height of 27 kilometers, the little Martians would be giants."
  **Today:** Olympus Mons was put at about 25–27 km from Viking-era data. Mars Global Surveyor's laser altimeter (MOLA, 1997–2001) measured about 21.9 km above the Martian datum, or about 26 km above the surrounding plains. "Big as France" is also generous: its roughly 600 km base covers about half the area of France.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Great quantities of this will be spread over the poles on Mars,"
  **Today:** Darkening the polar caps to release their CO₂ and warm Mars was Carl Sagan's 1973 proposal. Jakosky & Edwards (*Nature Astronomy*, 2018), using MAVEN, Mars Express and orbiter data, found that Mars no longer has enough accessible CO₂ to thicken its atmosphere usefully with known technology.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "where the sea flowed into a bottomless pit."
  **Today:** Educated 15th-century Europeans knew the Earth was round. The fears around Cape Bojador recorded by Zurara were shoals, violent currents, a "Sea of Darkness", a boiling tropical sea and not being able to sail back, not falling off the edge of the world.
  **Why it was believed:** The flat-earth-explorers myth spread from Washington Irving's 1828 Columbus biography. Jeffrey Burton Russell had debunked it in 1991, but it was still common in children's media.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1994)
- **Quote:** "is located between 60 and 100,000 kilometers from Earth,"
  **Today:** Mars is about 55–100 million km from Earth at close approaches and up to about 400 million km at the far side of its orbit. "Thousand" is probably a dub or transcription slip for "million". The "200 to 300 times our distance from the moon" that follows fits close approaches only.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "you'd see your fellow passengers aging before your merry eyes."
  **Today:** Under relativity, passengers on the same ship age normally relative to each other. Time dilation shows up only between the ship and people left behind. Tachyons ("300 times faster still") are purely hypothetical, have never been detected, and could not be used for travel without breaking causality.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- **Quote:** "Our flight's suborbital, and we'll have crossed the Atlantic in 25 minutes." Not by 2020. Concorde retired in 2003 and point-to-point suborbital travel is still only proposed. Robot butlers like "Nester" did not arrive either.
- **Quote:** "You press this button, and you're linked directly to the memory bank." Largely came true through laptops, tablets and the internet in schools. The 100,000-page "laser disc" was overtaken by networked storage, and classroom holograms did not become common.
- **Quote:** "It's an 800 kilometers in diameter." Probably a mishearing of about 6,800 km (actual 6,779 km), which fits "approximately two times smaller" than Earth.
- **Quote:** "3 or 4,000 kilometers in length," Valles Marineris is about 4,000 km long, up to about 200 km wide and 7 km deep. Fine.
- **Quote:** "Its atmosphere contains 95% carbon dioxide, deadly for living creatures." About 95–96% CO₂, and very thin. Correct.
- **Quote:** "the rising level of all the earth's oceans consequence of the greenhouse effect" Sea-level rise has continued as projected (about 20 cm since 1900, now about 4 mm per year). No World Assembly or floating cities.
- **Quote:** "Liberty 400 kilometers above the earth is almost operational" A good guess. The ISS (assembled from 1998) orbits at about 400–420 km, though it is not a launch base for interplanetary ships.
- **Quote:** "A nine-month journey one way plus the factor of the ellipsis 22 months" Minimum-energy transfers take about 7–9 months, and a full mission is about 1.5–3 years depending on the stay.
- **Quote:** "The production of red corpuscles will surely slow down and no white cells" This is an objector exaggerating. Spaceflight anaemia is real (Trudel et al., 2022, found red cells destroyed faster in space), as are bone loss and kidney-stone risk. White-cell production does not stop, and radiation raises cancer risk without making it certain. Artificial gravity has not been built.
- **Quote:** "if we succeed in towing even one small asteroid to" NASA's Asteroid Redirect Mission (2013) was cancelled in 2017. Asteroid mining is still speculative.
- **Quote:** "a preliminary exploration with robots will" This is what happened: Pathfinder (1997), Spirit and Opportunity, Curiosity, Perseverance. As of 2020 no crewed Mars mission had been approved.
- **Quote:** "Meanwhile, on a lunar base, we are stocking up on oxygen from the lunar rocks." Making oxygen from regolith is a real research goal. MOXIE made oxygen from CO₂ on Mars in 2021. There is no lunar base.
- **Quote:** "Our spaceships could develop a technique of harpooning, a sling effect." Gravity assists use planets. Asteroids are far too small to give a useful slingshot, and harpooning one would mean enormous accelerations. Treat it as fiction.
- **Quote:** "It is approaching us at a speed of 100,000 kilometers per hour." Within the real range of about 40,000–260,000 km/h. Saying any impact "would destroy all forms of life" overstates it: even the dinosaur-killing impact did not. Deflection has since been tested: NASA's DART spacecraft shortened Dimorphos's orbit by 33 minutes in 2022.
- **Quote:** "With its nuclear engine on its way toward the star Barnard six light years away," Barnard's Star is 5.96 light years away, the target of the British Interplanetary Society's Project Daedalus fusion probe study (1973–78).
- **Quote:** "that sail on lasers." Laser-pushed light sails became the Breakthrough Starshot concept (2016). Fusion and antimatter propulsion are still on paper.
- **Quote:** "A billion kilometers per hour." The speed of light is 1.08 billion km/h. Correct.
