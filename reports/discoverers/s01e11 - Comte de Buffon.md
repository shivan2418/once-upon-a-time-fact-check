# Once Upon a Time... The Discoverers — s01e11: Comte de Buffon
The episode asks how old the Earth is. It contrasts Archbishop Ussher's biblical date and other theories based on the Flood with Buffon's work: his needle probability problem, his burning mirrors, his theory of a comet striking the Sun, his censure by the Sorbonne, and his cooling iron balls that gave an age of about 75,000 years. A "time machine" then visits the early Earth, the Carboniferous, the break-up of the continents and the end of the dinosaurs. It teaches that the Earth is about 4.5 billion years old and that Buffon pioneered a scientific history of the Earth.

## Outdated (since 1994)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Well, it was 4,004 years B.C., the 29th of October."
  **Today:** Ussher (1650) dated Creation to the nightfall before Sunday **23** October 4004 BC. The "9 o'clock in the morning" comes from John Lightfoot of Cambridge (1644), who meant something different: the creation of man, at the autumn equinox. The two were merged in popular retellings. ("29th" may be a dub or transcription slip.)
  **Why it was believed:** The merged "23 October 4004 BC at 9 a.m." version was widely repeated in popular books and textbooks.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "No one, not even the scientists, could yet comprehend that the sacred book, the Bible, spoke in symbols and that religion and science need not be antagonistic."
  **Today:** Reading Genesis non-literally is very old: Augustine (*De Genesi ad litteram*, around 415) warned against using Scripture to contradict what is known about nature. Galileo argued the same (episode 9). Burnet and Whiston themselves treated parts of Genesis as figurative. The line, and the closing "The study was finally getting to replace theology", follows the science-versus-religion "conflict" story.
  **Why it was believed:** The Draper–White conflict narrative dominated popular history of science.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "This would be the first time a scientist asserted that the Earth had not been created in six days"
  **Today:** Buffon's 1778 figure was the first age *estimated by experiment*, which is the fair claim. Others had already rejected the six days and the 6,000 years. Benoît de Maillet's *Telliamed* (written in the 1720s, published 1748) gave an age of about two billion years. Descartes and Leibniz described a long natural cooling of the Earth, and Buffon himself had said "much more than 6,000 years" in 1749.
  **Why it was believed:** Buffon was the best-known early figure, and popular accounts made him "the first".
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Yes, he's the one, he gets all the glory for all our modern biology, Buffon."
  **Today:** Buffon was a major figure, but this overstates his role. He mostly held species to be fixed, with "degeneration" within a family (e.g. pig and boar), and he explicitly rejected common descent of all animals in 1753, though he toyed with the idea. Evolutionary theory owes much more to Lamarck (1809), Darwin and Wallace (1858–59). Linnaeus's classification also shaped modern biology. Jacques Roger (*Buffon*, 1989) gives the balanced view.
  **Why it was believed:** Older histories, e.g. in A. O. Lovejoy's *Forerunners of Darwin* (1959), cast Buffon as a forerunner of Darwin.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1994)
- **Quote:** "Nobody since Aristotle the Greek 2,000 years earlier had attempted that."
  **Today:** Pliny the Elder's 37-book *Natural History* (AD 77) is the obvious counter-example. Renaissance encyclopedists such as Conrad Gesner and Ulisse Aldrovandi also attempted complete natural histories.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- Archbishop James Ussher was Archbishop of Armagh, the head of the Church of Ireland: fine.
- Thomas Burnet (*Sacred Theory of the Earth*, 1681) was a clergyman, not really a scientist. His original Earth was smooth and featureless ("flat") until the Flood broke its crust: fair.
- William Whiston (1696) blamed the Flood on a comet: correct.
- Abbé Noël-Antoine Pluche (*Spectacle de la nature*, from 1732, and *Histoire du ciel*, 1739) gave a Flood-based Earth history. "Rattle the earth" is garbled; he had the Earth's axis tilted at the Deluge.
- Buffon's needle problem (1733), the first problem in geometric probability, is real. The bet with Maupertuis is invented.
- Elected to the Academy of Sciences in January 1734 at 26 (the episode says 27). His work on timber strength for the navy, and his appointment as Intendant of the Jardin du Roi (1739), are correct. "Monsieur de Bontemps" is Daubenton, his collaborator, working at Montbard.
- Buffon's burning mirror (1747), made of 168 flat mirrors, set wood alight at about 150 feet and melted metals in front of Louis XV, reviving Archimedes' mirrors against Descartes' doubts: correct.
- His 1749 theory that a comet struck the Sun and knocked off matter that formed the planets is correctly stated. It was later replaced by the nebular hypothesis.
- The Sorbonne censure (1751), the attack by the Abbé Lelarge de Lignac, and Buffon's retraction printed in 1753 are accurately described.
- Cooling iron balls (*Époques de la nature*, 1778) gave about 74,832 years. His manuscripts considered about 3 million years or more. "3,500,000" is close.
- The seven epochs are loosely paraphrased. Buffon's third epoch was a universal ocean, and life appeared then (marine shells). His sixth was the separation of the Old World from the New by sinking land, not continental drift as now understood.
- The Earth is about 4.5 billion years old (Patterson, 1956): correct. "Billion" here is explicitly a thousand million.
- Life around 3 billion years ago: fine, though microfossils and stromatolites reported before 1994 already pointed to at least 3.5 billion.
- The Carboniferous (about 359–299 million years ago) with coal forests and the first reptiles: fine. The first insects are older (Devonian), and the late Carboniferous had major glaciations.
- Pangaea breaking up around 180 million years ago, and the dinosaurs' end 66 million years ago (often given as 65 in 1994): correct.
