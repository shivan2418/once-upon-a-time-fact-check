# Once Upon a Time... Space — s01e13: The Incas
Maestro and the patrol visit a planet with a "pre-Incan" Andean-style civilisation. They are shown its storehouses, knotted-cord *quipu* records, rope bridges, *ayllu* communities, decimal chain of chiefs, *chasqui* relay runners and gold-working. A local legend tells of bearded sky-gods whose anger made a desert, and a palace faction steals the visitors' lasers before order is restored. Most of the real history comes from what is known of the Inca Empire.

## Wrong (even in 1982)
- **Quote:** "Your society is magnificent, exactly as depicted in all the accounts of prehistory on the Earth. It is an Indian civilization in the year 1000."
  **Today:** What is shown (the decimal chain of officials, *chasqui* relay runners, state storehouses, the *ayllu* system under an imperial chief) is the **Inca Empire**, which expanded from about 1438 and fell to the Spanish in 1532–33. Around 1000 AD the Andes were dominated by Tiwanaku and Wari, which had some similar features (Wari used khipus and roads) but not this system.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "These bellows are the largest, most powerful ever built"
  **Today:** Andean metalworkers had no bellows before the Spanish. They melted metal with blowpipes and wind-driven clay furnaces (*huayrachinas*) on hillsides, described by Spanish chroniclers such as José de Acosta. (The surrounding transcript is garbled.)
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- *Quipu* (knotted cords) as records of quantities; the colour code given (yellow for gold, etc.) follows the chronicler Garcilaso de la Vega and is only partly confirmed.
- *Ayllu* as the basic kin and land-sharing community, and the decimal hierarchy of officials responsible for 10, 100, 1,000 households and so on.
- *Chicha* (maize beer), llamas as pack animals, gold panned from rivers, and grass-rope suspension bridges plaited from smaller cords (like the Q'eswachaka bridge, still rebuilt every year).
- *Chasqui* relay runners covering "1,000 kilometers in less than three days": at the high end of chroniclers' estimates (about 240 km a day is often quoted), but plausible.
- The legend of pale, bearded sky-gods echoes the Viracocha myth and 1970s "ancient astronaut" ideas. It is told as a local legend, not as fact.
