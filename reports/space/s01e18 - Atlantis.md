# Once Upon a Time... Space — s01e18: Atlantis
Pierrot stays on Earth and is shown round its ocean farms, old drilling platforms and the underwater city of Poseidia. On a dive with Captain Pete he blacks out from rapture of the deep and sees a sunken city, and Pete then tells his "memories" of Atlantis: its arrival on Earth, its war of conquest around the Mediterranean and its sinking. Along the way the episode teaches the ocean food chain, plankton and photosynthesis, ocean thermal energy, water pressure, nitrogen narcosis and decompression, and Plato's account of Atlantis.

## Wrong (even in 1982)
- **Quote:** "No, you realize the pressure here is three tons per square centimeter."
  **Today:** At the stated depth of 7,000 m, seawater pressure is about 700 atmospheres (about 70 MPa), or roughly 0.7 tonnes per square centimetre. "Three tons" is about four times too high. Pressure rises by about 1 atmosphere (about 1 kg/cm²) every 10 m, which was textbook knowledge.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "Their presence accounts for the lapse of five million years"
  **Today:** There was no gap between Neanderthals and Cro-Magnons. The two overlapped in Europe: Cro-Magnons (early modern humans) arrived about 45,000–40,000 years ago, and the last Neanderthals died out about 40,000 years ago (about 35,000 in 1982 references). Five million years ago there were not even any members of the genus *Homo*.
  **Confidence:** high

## Simplified but fine
- Food chain "one kilogram of tuna fish required ten kilograms of sardines ... 100 kilograms of animal plankton ... 1,000 of vegetable plankton": the standard rough "10% rule" for energy passed between levels of a food chain.
- Ocean thermal energy, "a difference of some 20 degrees for each 1,000 meters of depth": OTEC plants do need about 20 °C between warm surface water and cold water from about 1,000 m. The wording "for each 1,000 meters" is off, though: the drop is not repeated per kilometre, and deep water stays at about 2–4 °C.
- "oh plant is what they really like to eat" (whales) is probably a mishearing of "plankton". Baleen whales eat animal plankton (krill, copepods) and small fish, not plant plankton.
- Plant plankton as the base of the ocean food chain, using photosynthesis to absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen: correct; it produces roughly half of Earth's oxygen.
- Metals "cobalt uranium nickel copper ... all from the sea": seabed manganese nodules are rich in nickel, copper and cobalt, and seawater holds dissolved uranium.
- Rapture of the deep: most divers breathing air "lose their judgment at less than 100 meters" is true (nitrogen narcosis usually starts around 30 m). The 80 m limit is generous for air but could fit a gas mix. Slow, staged decompression stops on the way up are correct.
- "Australopithecus, those evolved monkeys that peopled the Earth" over a million years ago: *Australopithecus* lived about 4–2 million years ago (robust relatives until about 1.2 million). They were early hominins (apes), not monkeys. The aliens guiding human evolution is the episode's fiction.
- Atlantis: the details match Plato (*Timaeus* and *Critias*): Egyptian priests told Solon of it, it was "greater than Libya and Asia" put together, it lay beyond the Pillars of Heracles ("On Gibraltar" is probably a mishearing of "beyond Gibraltar"), its army conquered lands around the Mediterranean before Athens defeated it, and it sank about 9,000 years before Solon (about 9600 BC, matching "9,560 years before our era"). Historians and archaeologists regard Atlantis as Plato's invention, and there is no evidence that it existed; the episode frames it as Pete's dreams and a "fanciful theory".
