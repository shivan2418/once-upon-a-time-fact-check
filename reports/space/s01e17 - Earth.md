# Once Upon a Time... Space — s01e17: Earth
Pierrot, Psyche and Metro take a holiday on a future Earth ringed by solar mirrors and orbiting junk. The old professor tells them its imagined history: resources running out, overpopulation, genetic engineering, the war of 2183, a "third Renaissance", computer rule and weather wars. On a tour of geostationary orbit they find a saboteur at the mirror ring. Most of it is invented future history. The real science covers geostationary orbit, the ionosphere blocking long radio waves, the Sun as the source of Earth's energy, how much solar power reaches Earth, space debris, and a quick look at the Greek and Italian Renaissances.

## Outdated (since 1982)
- **Quote:** "As early as the year 2000, there were 400 objects in the sky,"
  **Today:** Given as a prediction for 2000, and much too low. In 1982 the US already tracked about 5,000 objects in Earth orbit. By 2000 the figure was about 9,000, with several hundred to about a thousand near geostationary altitude alone. Today more than 30,000 are tracked. If the line meant only working geostationary satellites, about 300 by 2000, it was closer.

## Simplified but fine
- "36,000 kilometers ... the so-called geostatic zone" and later "35,860 kilometers": geostationary orbit is 35,786 km above the equator, where an orbit takes one sidereal day and a satellite appears fixed in the sky.
- Radio telescopes placed "above the ionosphere since long-wave radio frequencies didn't penetrate that layer": correct; the ionosphere blocks frequencies below about 10–30 MHz.
- "100,000 billion kilometers ... only 10,000 light years": consistent only with the long-scale billion (10^17 km is about 10,600 light years). With the short-scale billion it would be about 10.6 light years.
- "250 trillion horsepower" of sunlight: Earth intercepts about 1.7 × 10^17 W, about 230 trillion horsepower.
- "Everything comes from the sun ... petroleum oil and wood": true for fossil fuels, wood, wind and hydro (not nuclear, geothermal or tidal).
- The world population of about 6 billion in 2000 ("the poorest billion human beings the year 2000", probably with a number dropped in the transcript) was right (6.1 billion). "Over 30 billion" by 2100 is the story's own forecast; UN projections give about 10 billion.
- "A bomb, a couple of megatons ... weapons thousands of times more powerful": megaton-range weapons were real (the largest tested was 50 Mt). "Thousands of times" more is the story's future.
- The first "Renaissance" of Pericles' Athens, when "man ... was the measure of all things" (a saying of Protagoras, not Socrates), and the Italian Quattrocento about 1,900 years later with Leonardo and Michelangelo. "Pranuti" is probably a mishearing of Brunelleschi.
- Uranus and Saturn have rings: correct (Uranus's were found in 1977; Jupiter's, found in 1979, go unmentioned).
