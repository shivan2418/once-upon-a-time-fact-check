# Once Upon a Time... Space — s01e11: Shipwrecked in Space
A cruiser damaged by the supernova crash-lands on an uncharted green planet. While the crew struggles to survive, Pierrot, Psyche and Metro explore, meet friendly telepathic "Patakis" and dangerous ape-like "Zabus", and find Cassiopeian prospectors; a cowardly officer, Sanders, wants to surrender to them. Real science comes in brief remarks about Earth's prehistoric animals, mammals and radio and the ionosphere.

## Wrong (even in 1982)
- **Quote:** "But consider, it wasn't so very long ago, a few hundred million years on Earth, the creatures didn't look much like you either. I recall to you the Splendous Clutosaurus and the Demetrodon and the Cignathosaurus. Very nice in its way. And there is also the Moa and the Tyrannosaurus."
  **Today:** Dimetrodon (about 295–272 million years ago), Cynognathus (about 245–235 million years ago) and Tyrannosaurus (about 68–66 million years ago) fit the "hundreds of millions of years" idea, but the moa does not. It was a giant flightless bird in New Zealand, hunted to extinction by the Māori about 600 years ago (around 1400 AD). "Splendous Clutosaurus" is unidentifiable (a speech-recognition garble).

## Simplified but fine
- "Mammals of the plasticine [Pleistocene] era": a reasonable label for Ice Age-looking mammals.
- "All mammals are edible ... because their biochemical bases are the same": a broad rule of thumb; a few mammal tissues are toxic (e.g. polar bear liver, from too much vitamin A).
- "This is a relatively old planet. There isn't one active volcano here": reasonable reasoning, since small or old worlds lose internal heat.
- A short-wave transmitter whose signal is trapped below the ionosphere, so it needs a relay in orbit: broadly correct radio physics.
