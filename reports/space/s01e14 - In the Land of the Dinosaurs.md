# Once Upon a Time... Space — s01e14: In the Land of the Dinosaurs
Pierrot and Psyche crash-land on a volcanic planet that is in its "age of dinosaurs" and meet Allosaurus, Deinonychus, Brontosaurus, Stegosaurus, Megalosaurus, Tyrannosaurus and the sea reptile Tylosaurus while Metro repairs the ship. Along the way they discuss the "primeval soup" and the origin of life, dinosaur sizes, how long dinosaurs ruled, and why they died out (Metro favours a nearby supernova).

## Outdated (since 1982)
- **Quote:** "There are several explanations for that. Lack of food... Climactic changes. The appearance of glaciers. But the most probable explanation is a supernova explosion so near that its cosmic radiation killed most of life on the planet."
  **Today:** The end-Cretaceous extinction (66 million years ago) is now attributed mainly to an asteroid impact, proposed by Alvarez et al. in 1980 from the iridium layer and confirmed when the Chicxulub crater in Mexico was identified in 1990–91. Massive Deccan Traps volcanism probably also contributed. The supernova idea (Schindewolf 1950s; Russell & Tucker 1971) was a serious candidate in the 1970s, but Alvarez's team had already found no plutonium-244 in the boundary clay, which a supernova would have left. There were no glaciers at the end of the Cretaceous; the climate was warm.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "and then, one day they all disappeared. They died out."
  **Today:** "They all disappeared" is outdated: birds are living dinosaurs. John Ostrom argued this in the 1970s, it became the consensus in the 1990s, and feathered dinosaurs from China (Sinosauropteryx, 1996, and many since) confirmed it. Only the non-bird dinosaurs died out.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1982)
- **Quote:** "Planet formed out of dense gaseous clouds when its sun was formed 400 billion years ago."
  **Today:** The universe is about 13.8 billion years old (in 1982 estimates were about 10–20 billion), so no star or planet can be 400 billion years old. With the old British long-scale billion it is even worse. Earth is 4.54 billion years old. Probably a slip for 4 billion.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "Primeval soup brewed over 100 million years ago, sufficient to produce life."
  **Today:** On Earth, life appeared at least 3.5 billion years ago, and getting from the first cells to large animals such as dinosaurs took more than 3 billion years. A world where life began only 100 million years ago could not yet have dinosaurs.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "ruled over it for 30 million years"
  **Today:** Dinosaurs appeared about 230 million years ago and the non-bird dinosaurs died out 66 million years ago, about 165 million years in all. Books in 1982 already gave about 140–160 million years.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "those beauties weigh 10 tons apiece" (Allosaurus)
  **Today:** Allosaurus weighed about 1.5–2.5 tonnes (large individuals perhaps up to about 4). Books in 1982 gave a similar 2–4 tonnes. Ten tonnes is closer to Tyrannosaurus.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "the same men were very wise in my opinion to appear on earth a hundred million years after these nice moving mountains had disappeared"
  **Today:** The last non-bird dinosaurs (including sauropods related to "Brontosaurus") died out 66 million years ago, and the genus *Homo* appeared about 2.5–2.8 million years ago. The gap is about 63–64 million years, not 100 million.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen as the building blocks of amino acids in a "primeval soup": the Oparin–Haldane idea and the Miller–Urey experiment (1953). How life began is still unresolved.
- "Brontosaurus. He weighs at least 30 tons ... herbivorous": the right size range (about 15–30 tonnes). In 1982 the name was officially a synonym of *Apatosaurus*, but Tschopp et al. (2015) revived *Brontosaurus* as a valid genus.
- Brontosaurus herds with adults guarding young in the middle: supported by sauropod trackways, though herd structure is still debated.
- The planet is called "Jurassic", but the animals mix Jurassic (Allosaurus, Stegosaurus, Brontosaurus, Megalosaurus) and Late Cretaceous (Tyrannosaurus, Tylosaurus) species. These are about 80 million years apart on Earth; this is acceptable on an alien world. Tylosaurus was a mosasaur, a marine reptile, not a dinosaur.
- Deinonychus, Stegosaurus and Megalosaurus are named correctly.
