# Once Upon a Time... Space — s01e15: The Rings of Saturn
Psyche takes a holiday trip with Metro to test the theory that the asteroid belt is a planet destroyed in an ancient war. She flies past Uranus and through Saturn's rings, is captured by humanoid machines at a secret base and interrogated. Pierrot rescues her, and they escape past Jupiter into the asteroid belt. Along the way the episode gives many facts about Uranus, Saturn and its rings and moons, Jupiter and its moons, and the asteroids.

## Outdated (since 1982)
- **Quote:** "That's Encelade. One of its moons, Saturn has 17 moons in all."
  **Today:** Seventeen was about right after Voyager (1980–81). Since then telescope surveys (Gladman, Sheppard, Jewitt and others, from 2000) and the Cassini mission have raised the count to 274 confirmed moons (as of 2025), most of them small irregular moons.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "You see that dark spot? Several Earths could fit inside it."
  **Today:** In the late 1970s and early 1980s Jupiter's Great Red Spot was about 25,000–40,000 km long, room for two to three Earths side by side. It has been shrinking steadily, and by the 2020s it is only about 14,000 km across, roughly one Earth.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "Where are the other satellites? They're 14 in all, if I am not mistaken."
  **Today:** In 1982 sixteen were known (Voyager added Metis, Adrastea and Thebe in 1979–80), so 14 was already a few years old. Surveys since 1999, led by Scott Sheppard's team, have raised the total to about 97 (2025).
  **Confidence:** high

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Width, 60,000 kilometers. Thickness, only 16 kilometers. Rather like a cigarette paper."
  **Today:** The width of the main rings (about 62,000 km) is right, but they are typically only about 10 metres to 1 km thick. Voyager measurements in 1980–81 (stellar occultations) already showed this; Cassini (2004–17) confirmed it.
  **Why it was believed:** Older popular books often gave "about 10 miles" (16 km) as the thickness.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Mimas, closest to the planet. A mere 180,000 kilometers."
  **Today:** Mimas orbits at about 185,500 km. It has not been the innermost known moon since Janus (1966) and Epimetheus; Voyager added Atlas, Prometheus and Pandora (1980), and Cassini found more. The episode's own count of 17 includes these inner moons.
  **Why it was believed:** For a century Mimas was the innermost known moon, and older books still said so.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "In fact, the others don't have names. They're referred to by numbers."
  **Today:** The IAU officially named Jupiter's outer moons (Himalia, Elara, Pasiphae, Sinope, Lysithea, Carme, Ananke, Leda) in 1975. The three Voyager moons were still awaiting names in 1982 (Metis, Adrastea and Thebe were named in 1983). So the remark was half-true in 1982 and is wrong today; all of Jupiter's moons have names except the newest small finds, which carry provisional designations.
  **Why it was believed:** Before 1975 most textbooks listed the outer moons only as Jupiter VI–XII.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "But, according to other theories, a planet did exist there once."
  **Today:** The idea that the asteroids are a broken-up planet (Olbers, 1802) had been dropped by specialists long before 1982. All the asteroids together hold only about 3–4% of the Moon's mass, and their varied compositions point to material that Jupiter's gravity stopped from ever forming a planet. (The "galactic war" is the episode's fiction.)
  **Why it was believed:** The exploded-planet story was a favourite in older popular books and science fiction.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1982)
- **Quote:** "Mass, 100 times that of the Earth." (Uranus)
  **Today:** Uranus has about 14.5 Earth masses, and its volume is about 63 Earths. This was well known long before 1982.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "A hundred million years. Well, that's how long it is from the time the dinosaurs disappeared and the true Homo erectus appeared."
  **Today:** The non-bird dinosaurs died out 66 million years ago (65 million in 1982 references), and *Homo erectus* appeared about 1.9 million years ago. The gap is about 64 million years.
  **Confidence:** high

## Simplified but fine
- The asteroid belt "between Jupiter and Mars, some 500 million kilometers from Earth": within the actual range (the belt lies about 2.2–3.3 AU from the Sun).
- Uranus "green because its layer of methane absorbs" red light (it looks blue-green); "nights 42 years long, and the days too" is correct at the poles (84-year orbit, axis tilted 98°); its rings (found in 1977) are dark ice and rock; Oberon, Ariel and Umbriel are named correctly.
- Saturn "after Jupiter, the largest ... 800 times the size of Earth" (about 760 Earth volumes), mostly hydrogen and helium, and less dense than water.
- "Thousand rings formed out of rock and ice": Voyager showed thousands of ringlets; they are over 95% water ice, with little rock. The Cassini Division and an outer diameter of about 275,000 km (A ring about 273,600 km) are right.
- "Titan, the largest of the satellites. Larger than the planet Mercury": true for Saturn, and larger than Mercury in diameter (5,150 vs 4,880 km). Jupiter's Ganymede is the largest moon in the solar system.
- Jupiter "1,300 times the size of Earth" (volume), the strongest planetary radio source, "Europa, the icy one", "Io, the volcanic" (Voyager, 1979).
- Ceres "diameter of 1,000 kilometers" (about 940 km), and Pallas as the next largest.
- Nietzsche's "live dangerously" (*The Gay Science*, §283) is a real quote; the "equal of God" framing is the dub's.
