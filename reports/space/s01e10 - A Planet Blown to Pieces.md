# Once Upon a Time... Space — s01e10: A Planet Blown to Pieces
Cassiopeia leaves the confederation, and Maestro warns that the star next to its new Triton base is about to become a supernova. Along the way he lectures on the size of the Milky Way, distances to nearby stars, how supernovae work, famous historical supernovae, other galaxies, and the Sun's future as a red giant. The star explodes, and Omega's ships fight through Cassiopeian fire to evacuate the colonists.

## Outdated (since 1982)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Yes, in the galaxy NC-34, the constellation of the Great Bear, an explosion pulverized well over a million stars in a few seconds."
  **Today:** "NC-34" is almost certainly NGC 3034, i.e. M82 in Ursa Major. Lynds and Sandage (1963) described it as an "exploding galaxy" with a huge outburst from its core. By the late 1970s–80s it was understood as a **starburst** galaxy: intense star formation, probably set off by a close pass of M81, drives a "superwind" of gas out of the galaxy. No event destroyed a million stars in seconds, and no supernova could.
  **Why it was believed:** The "exploding galaxy M82" image from the 1960s stayed in popular astronomy books for years.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1982)
- **Quote:** "our own galaxy there in the Milky Way extends over a distance equivalent to that covered in 100 million light years."
  **Today:** The Milky Way's disc is about 100,000 light years across (often given today as 100,000–120,000 ly or more). That was the standard figure in 1982 as well. "Million" is probably a slip for "thousand".
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "And as for Auriga, it's only 47 light years, and Alderbarra, 43."
  **Today:** Capella (Alpha Aurigae) at about 43 ly (about 45 ly in 1982 references) is fine. Aldebaran is about 65 ly away (Hipparcos, 1997), and 1982 references gave about 68 ly, so 43 ly is wrong. The two figures may have been swapped or misread.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Its outer shell is 15 times colder than its center."
  **Today:** In a massive star near the end of its life, the core reaches billions of kelvin while the surface is a few thousand kelvin, a ratio of roughly a million, not 15.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "At that point, it will incorporate its satellites, Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, and Pluto."
  **Today:** As a red giant the Sun is expected to swell to about 1 AU at most. Mercury and Venus will be swallowed; Earth is borderline (studies such as Schröder & Smith 2008 suggest it is engulfed). Mars, Jupiter, Saturn and Pluto, at 1.5 to 40 AU, will not be. The models known in 1982 gave the same picture. (The list also oddly leaves out Uranus and Neptune.)
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "But be assured, this will not happen for another five million years."
  **Today:** The Sun has about 5 **billion** years left before it becomes a red giant. Astronomers already said so in 1982. Probably a translation slip (French *milliards*).
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "In 1054, the Crab Nebula, in 1752, there was one in the Cassopoeia region, 1604, another one in the Neblia Officius."
  **Today:** 1054 (Crab) and 1604 (Kepler's supernova, in Ophiuchus) are right. The Cassiopeia supernova is Tycho's, in **1572**. No supernova was seen in 1752. This may be a dub or speech-recognition error.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- "The mean distance lying between the center of the galaxy ... and the solar system is in the region of 27,000 light years": close to today's about 26,000–26,700 ly (GRAVITY 2019). In 1982 the IAU value was still 10 kpc (32,600 ly), but about 8.5 kpc (27,700 ly) was already being proposed and was adopted in 1985.
- "Centaur is very near the sun, four light years": Alpha Centauri is about 4.4 ly away.
- A star running out of fuel and collapsing, briefly shining like billions of suns, and throwing off much of its mass at about 10,000 km/s: a fair summary of a core-collapse supernova. At that speed debris would cross 5 billion km in about 6 days; the episode says "some eight days".
- "Only in the 20th century ... it was realized that ... [many objects] are, in fact, independent galaxies": Hubble's Cepheids in Andromeda (1924–25). Some galaxies are much bigger than the Milky Way, but Andromeda is only about the same size or somewhat larger, not ten times larger.
- The red giant growing "millions and millions of times" in size works if it means volume (a radius about 100–250 times larger).
- "In 2421, another one that was recorded in the region of Urn": the series is set in the future, so this is a fictional future supernova, not an error.
