# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e02: Alexander the Great
The episode follows Alexander from taming Bucephalus and his lessons with Aristotle, through his quarrel with Philip, to his campaign against Persia. It covers the Granicus, the Gordian knot, Issus, Tyre and Gaza, Egypt, Siwa and Alexandria, then Gaugamela, Babylon and Susa. It ends with his growing excesses, the killing of Philotas and Parmenio, and his death at 33, and presents him as a spreader of Greek thought who was also ruined by his passions.

## Outdated (since 1996)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "The Persians could raise an army of a million men."
  **Today:** "A million men" comes from ancient writers: Herodotus on Xerxes, and Arrian on Gaugamela. Modern historians estimate the largest Persian field armies of Alexander's time at roughly 50,000–120,000. The episode repeats the figure later ("He said he has a million soldiers"). The river-drinking story is Herodotus' and is fairly framed with "it was said".
  **Why it was believed:** Popular retellings followed the ancient numbers, even though scholars had long rejected them.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** major

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "It extended right to India, China, Russia, all the way to Egypt."
  **Today:** The Achaemenid empire reached the Indus, Central Asia (Sogdiana) and Egypt. It never bordered or included China, and apart from a foothold north of the Caucasus it did not reach what is now Russia. The list of modern countries that follows (Afghanistan, Pakistan, Iran, Iraq, Syria, Israel, Lebanon, Turkey) is correct.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "Ptolemy, you will take the right."
  **Today:** At the Granicus (334 BC), and in all his major battles, Alexander led the Companion cavalry on the **right** wing himself, and Parmenio held the left. Ptolemy, son of Lagus, was not yet a senior commander. The sun-in-their-eyes reasoning is invented. The real story is that Parmenio advised waiting until dawn and Alexander attacked across the river at once, late in the day.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "gaza was another hard nut to crack greek fire it was a novelty"
  **Today:** "Greek fire" was invented about 1,000 years later, around AD 672, by the Byzantines (traditionally by Kallinikos). The defenders of Tyre in 332 BC did use fire ships and heated sand, but not Greek fire.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "And the faithful Parmenio will take the last enemy stronghold on the way to the fabulous Persepolis."
  **Today:** The last obstacle before Persepolis was the Persian Gates, held by Ariobarzanes. Alexander took the pass himself in early 330 BC by outflanking it on a mountain path. Parmenio led the baggage and heavy troops by the slower main road.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- Bucephalus ("Cephalus") was afraid of his own shadow. This is Plutarch's anecdote.
- Macedonia lay just north of the Greek city-states.
- Philip hired Aristotle as tutor in 343 BC. The curved shadow of the Earth on the Moon in a lunar eclipse is Aristotle's real argument for a round Earth (*On the Heavens*).
- Alexander claimed descent from Achilles through his mother and from Heracles through his father. Achilles was a mortal hero, not a god, but the line is the boy's boast and Aristotle corrects him.
- Philip married Cleopatra Eurydice (337 BC), Attalus insulted Alexander, Philip drew his sword and fell "passing from couch to couch", and Alexander fled. All of this follows Plutarch.
- Olympias' story that Zeus was Alexander's father, and the Diogenes "you're blocking the sun" exchange, are ancient anecdotes.
- Sparta refused to join the League of Corinth ("not us Spartans").
- Alexander crossed to Asia with about 32,000 infantry and 5,000 cavalry, close to the show's 30,000. The 100,000 Persians at the Granicus is Diodorus' figure.
- Apelles (painter) and Lysippus (sculptor) were Alexander's official portraitists.
- Gordion, Midas, Gordius' ox-cart, the oracle that whoever undid the knot would rule Asia, and Alexander cutting it are all in the ancient sources.
- Issus (333 BC) followed Darius ("Sidereus") marching behind Alexander. The Damascus haul is close to Parmenio's list in Athenaeus: 329 concubine-musicians, 277 cooks and 46 garland-weavers.
- Tyre and Gaza (332 BC), Alexandria (331 BC), the oracle of Zeus-Ammon at Siwa ("Sionat-Amon"), Gaugamela ("Abridis", that is, Arbela, 331 BC), the welcome at Babylon, and the surrender of Susa ("Caesar").
- Alexander did see naphtha (oil) seeps in Babylonia, as Plutarch reports. The "black gold" line is a joke.
- Philotas was executed and Parmenio assassinated (330 BC). The "you're not a god" scene suggests Cleitus (328 BC) or the proskynesis quarrel with Callisthenes.
- Alexander died at Babylon in June 323 BC, a few weeks short of 33. The cause (fever such as malaria, or poison) is still unknown.
- "Greatest empire ... ever known": Alexander's empire roughly matched the Achaemenid empire it took over.
- Greek thought spread across the Hellenistic East and later shaped both Rome and Arabic philosophy. Rome itself grew outside Alexander's conquests.
