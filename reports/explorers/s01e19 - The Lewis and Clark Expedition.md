# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e19: The Lewis and Clark Expedition
Jefferson sends Meriwether Lewis, with William Clark and Clark's servant York, to explore the West just as France sells Louisiana to the United States. The episode follows the Corps of Discovery up the Missouri: the standoff with the Teton Sioux, a Mandan buffalo dance, the hiring of Charbonneau and Sacagawea, the meeting with the Shoshone, the starving crossing of the Bitterroots and the Nez Perce who saved them, ending with a warning that the promised "peace and friendship" did not last.

## Outdated (since 1996)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "People, I know the passage."
  **Today:** Sacagawea was not the expedition's guide. She was taken on mainly as a Shoshone interpreter (with her husband Charbonneau) and as a sign of peaceful intent. She recognised a few landmarks near her homeland, such as Beaverhead Rock and later Bozeman Pass. The route over the Rockies was found with the Shoshone guide Old Toby and the Nez Perce. Lewis and Clark's journals make this clear.
  **Why it was believed:** Eva Emery Dye's novel *The Conquest* (1902) and the women's suffrage movement turned Sacagawea into "the guide who led Lewis and Clark", and that version filled children's books and films for most of the century.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "We must go around the hills of the Rocky Mountains, with peaks so high no man had ever crossed them."
  **Today:** The Lolo Trail was an established Nez Perce road that the Nez Perce used to cross to the buffalo plains. The Shoshone told Lewis about it, and Old Toby led the Corps along it. The mountains were new only to Euro-Americans. The later line "No man's ever gone up" repeats the same idea.
  **Why it was believed:** Frontier stories cast the West as untrodden wilderness and counted only white travellers as "firsts".
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "200 million acres and only a few cents per acre."
  **Today:** The Louisiana Purchase covered about 530 million acres (about 828,000 square miles, 2.1 million km²). The price was $15 million, about 3 cents an acre. "A few cents" is right, but the acreage is less than half the real figure.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "He says that alcohol is no good medicine."
  **Today:** The scene is the Teton Sioux encounter of September 1804, and the jealous second chief matches the Partisan's rivalry with Black Buffalo. But the Teton chiefs drank the whiskey they were offered on the keelboat. The well-known refusal of alcohol came from the Arikara a few weeks later, who said white men would not give them anything that made them fools if they were friends. The show has moved the Arikara story to the Teton.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "my own tribe one day sold me to him"
  **Today:** Sacagawea was not sold by the Shoshone. Hidatsa raiders captured her as a girl, around 1800, at the Three Forks of the Missouri. Charbonneau later got her from the Hidatsa, by purchase or, according to some accounts, by gambling. The transcript may have garbled "a tribe" or "another tribe".
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Clark had found the Northwest Passage, a land route to the Pacific"
  **Today:** The expedition's main geographic finding was that there was *no* Northwest Passage, meaning no easy water link between the Missouri and the Columbia. The crossing included about 220 miles of land carriage over very hard mountains. In his 1806 report Lewis did call this "the most practicable route" across the continent, but traders never used it, and the later Oregon Trail went through South Pass.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- "16 states": the Union had 16 states until Ohio was admitted in early 1803, which is when the expedition was being planned.
- British North America, New Spain, Spanish Florida, and Oregon claimed by Spain, Britain and Russia (and soon the US) are all right for 1803. "French Louisiana" was French again on paper after 1800, though Spain still ran it until 1803.
- The expedition was planned for New Orleans and the West before anyone knew Napoleon would sell all of Louisiana. The deal was negotiated by the *American* envoys in Paris (Livingston and Monroe). "Ambassador of France" is a dub slip.
- "Three times bigger than France": the Purchase was closer to four times France's area.
- "20 well-armed men": the Corps grew to about 33 permanent members (about 45 on the first leg).
- York, Clark's enslaved servant, did go. "Monsieur Drillet" is George Drouillard, the hunter and sign-language interpreter. "Titan Zoo" means the Teton Sioux (Lakota).
- Lewis's prefabricated iron-frame boat, the "Experiment", really was carried west and failed at the Great Falls in 1805. The show jokes about this at the end.
- The Mandan buffalo dance was seen in winter 1804–05. Sacagawea's name was understood as "Bird Woman" from Hidatsa.
- Charbonneau's total pay at the end really was about $500 ($500.33).
- The keelboat was sent back from Fort Mandan in 1805, and the Corps went on in pirogues and dugout canoes.
- Lewis waving a blanket and shouting "tab-ba-bone" to the Shoshone comes straight from his journal. The Lemhi Shoshone had indeed never seen white men, and horses were bought with shirts, knives and a gun.
- The starving crossing of the Bitterroots, and the Nez Perce who fed the Corps and helped it make canoes, are accurate.
- To its credit, the episode ends by noting that the promised peace with the tribes did not last.
