# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e18: Alexander von Humboldt
The episode follows Alexander von Humboldt and Aimé Bonpland from their meeting in Paris in 1798 and Carlos IV's permission. It covers their Venezuelan journey (electric eels, the cow tree, the Apure, the Orinoco and the Casiquiare, Bonpland's fever), the Andes and the attempt on Chimborazo, the cold Pacific current, and the meeting with Jefferson in 1804. Throughout, it stresses Humboldt as a holistic "ecologist" and an outspoken opponent of slavery, ending with *Kosmos* and the Berlin revolution of 1848.

## Outdated (since 1996)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Nobody has ever climbed higher."
  **Today:** Humboldt's party reached about 5,875 m on Chimborazo on 23 June 1802. That was the highest recorded climb by a European, and it stood for about 30 years. Centuries earlier, however, the Incas had climbed several Andean peaks over 6,000 m and built shrines on top: Llullaillaco (6,739 m), with summit ruins found in 1952, and Ampato, where the "Juanita" mummy was found in 1995. The "record" counts European climbers only.
  **Why it was believed:** Mountaineering history was written as a list of European "firsts", and Inca high-altitude archaeology was little known outside specialists.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "All those mosquitoes, they carry the disease."
  **Today:** This is anachronistic in 1800. Ronald Ross showed that mosquitoes spread malaria in 1897. Humboldt's era blamed "miasma" (bad air) from swamps.
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Only a Frenchman, Monsieur de la Condamine, published a book about the area."
  **Today:** Many earlier accounts existed. Jorge Juan and Antonio de Ulloa's *Relación histórica* (1748), from La Condamine's own expedition, was widely translated. The Jesuit José Gumilla's *El Orinoco ilustrado* (1741) and Gilij's *Saggio di storia americana* (1780s) covered the Orinoco itself.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "The human air rises over the mountains, the Andes going to the east."
  **Today:** The explanation has the weather running the wrong way. The coastal desert of Peru exists because the cold Humboldt Current cools the air above it. That cool air sits stable under warmer air (an inversion), so it gives fog (*garúa*) but almost no rain. Moisture over the Andes comes from the *east*: trade winds carry it from the Atlantic and the Amazon, and it rains on the eastern slopes. Pacific air does not cross the Andes and rain on the other side. The coast lies in the Andes' rain shadow. ("human" is a mishearing of "humid".)
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** major
- **Quote:** "At the age of 90, Humboldt completed the last volume of his great work, Cosmos."
  **Today:** Humboldt died on 6 May 1859 at 89, before finishing *Kosmos*. Volume 4 appeared in 1858, and volume 5 was put together from his papers and published after his death (1862).
  **Confidence:** high
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- "She knew, for example, in Venezuela, in the USA, in the Humboldt, Greenland, Mount Hum, and New Caledonia.": places named after Humboldt include Pico Humboldt in Venezuela, Humboldt County in the US, the Humboldt Glacier in Greenland and Mont Humboldt in New Caledonia.
- "This is Paris in the year 1798.": Humboldt met Bonpland (born in La Rochelle, trained in medicine) in Paris in 1798. Carlos IV gave them passports at Aranjuez in March 1799, and Humboldt paid for the expedition from his inheritance ("But sire, the Baron von Humboldt is quite prepared to finance the expedition entirely").
- "Someone here in the prime of life, admire the man's teeth.": Humboldt described with disgust buyers checking slaves' teeth at the Cumaná market. His opposition to slavery is well documented, for example in his *Political Essay on the Island of Cuba* (1826).
- "This is really extraordinary. An electric shock.": the electric eels and horses at Calabozo (March 1800) are accurate. Two horses drowned after being stunned. "It's a milk treat, delicious, not poison.": the cow tree (*Brosimum*) is accurate.
- "This river is the Apurri. It will lead to the Orinoco. It will take a few days.": the Apure is right. "Brother Zeer" is Father Bernardo Zea, who went with them.
- "Casquiare Canal." / "Condamine in his early exploration said that there was a canal.": correct. Humboldt confirmed and mapped the Casiquiare in 1800. Jesuits (Manuel Román, 1744) and the Spanish boundary commission already knew it, and to its credit the show does not say Humboldt discovered it.
- "More than 2,000 kilometers in this strange land.": the Orinoco loop was about 2,750 km.
- "We'll return to Angostura. You'll be alright.": Bonpland did fall dangerously ill at Angostura. "Quickly, prepare the quinine." is a little anachronistic: they would have used cinchona bark (*quinquina*), since quinine was isolated only in 1820.
- The episode skips the stay in Cuba (1800–01) and the trip up the Magdalena to Bogotá.
- "7,000 kilometers long, the peaks are more than 6,000 meters up.": correct for the Andes. "So, leaving from Quito, the ancient Inca capital, 2,500 meters above sea level,": Quito is at about 2,850 m.
- "Goes up 6,000 meters, a mountain no one's been able to climb.": Chimborazo is 6,263 m. The local porters turned back, and altitude sickness (bleeding gums and lips) forced the party down a few hundred metres below the summit.
- "It was right here the Inca Emperor at Hualpa": the Atahualpa ransom-room story at Cajamarca is accurate, and Humboldt visited it in 1802.
- "That's why this particular region has little rainfall and is as out as a desert.": linking the cold coastal current to the coastal desert is right. Humboldt measured the current's temperature, though the explanation that follows is garbled (see above).
- "Washington, the brand new capital of the United States, which has just won its independence.": Humboldt and Bonpland visited Jefferson in 1804, when Washington had been the capital since 1800 and independence was 21 years old. Humboldt's anti-slavery view is real, but "You cannot maintain your democracy when you put human beings in slavery because they have black skin." is a dramatised speech, not a recorded exchange.
- "The people of Berlin are rising up against injustice, shooting in the streets.": in March 1848 the aged Humboldt walked in the funeral procession for the revolutionaries killed in the street fighting.
- "Humanist, liberal, founder of ethnology and anthropology, the study of human society,": Humboldt was a pioneer, but "founder" overstates it. He is better called a founder of modern geography and of ecological plant geography.
