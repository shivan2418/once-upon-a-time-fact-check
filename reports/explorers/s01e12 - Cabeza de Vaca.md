# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e12: Cabeza de Vaca
The episode follows Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca, treasurer of Pánfilo de Narváez's 1528 Florida expedition. It covers the march to Apalachee, the improvised boats, the shipwreck on the Texas coast, years living among Native peoples as a slave, trader and healer, and the long walk with three companions to Spanish Mexico in 1536. It contrasts his respect for Native people with the slave-hunting of other conquistadors.

## Outdated (since 1996)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Imprisoned because he loved the Indians too much, he will rot away almost until the end of his days."
  **Today:** Cabeza de Vaca was governor of Río de la Plata from 1540. In 1544 he was overthrown by colonists under Domingo de Irala and sent to Spain in chains. In 1551 the Council of the Indies stripped him of office and banished him to Oran, but the sentence was later eased. He lived in Seville and died around 1559, apparently with some royal support. He was not imprisoned for the rest of his life. His protection of the Guaraní did anger the colonists, but the formal charges were abuse of power.
  **Why it was believed:** His own *Comentarios* (1555) cast him as a wronged protector of the Indians, and popular biographies followed that account.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "And then, I remind you that I am the one in charge."
  **Today:** The episode shows Narváez on land with the castaways after a month with a friendly people, planning to enslave them. Narváez was never with the survivors on Malhado (Galveston area). In November 1528, off the Texas coast, he refused to help the other boats ("every man for himself") and was later swept out to sea and lost.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "He discovered and described with passion the most unknown regions ranging from Florida to California and Mexico."
  **Today:** The survivors never reached California. Their route ran along the Gulf coast, across Texas and through northern Mexico (Chihuahua and Sonora) to Culiacán in Sinaloa, near the Gulf of California.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "It took Cabesa and his companions six years to cross"
  **Today:** The journey took eight years, from the April 1528 landing in Florida to April 1536, when they met Spaniards in Sinaloa. Most of that time (1528–34) they were held or working as traders along the Texas coast. The actual crossing of the continent took about two years, from 1534 to 1536.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- In 1527–28, about 15 years after Ponce de León (1513), Narváez landed near Tampa Bay. Cabeza de Vaca was his treasurer and provost. Cortés had beaten Narváez at Cempoala in 1520, where he lost an eye.
- Narváez split from the ships against advice, and the ships never found the land party again. The show's slave-raiding Narváez fits his reputation.
- Apalachee ("Appalachian") was in the Florida panhandle, not "over the hills". The Appalachian Mountains were later named after it. The "about 40 small houses", the attacks, and Aute nine days away "rich in corn, beans and fish" are taken straight from Cabeza de Vaca.
- The boats are right: they melted stirrups, spurs and crossbows in a forge, a Greek, Doroteo Teodoro, made pitch, and five boats were finished in about seven weeks (August–September 1528).
- The castaways' fear of being sacrificed, the women carrying loads, bride-price paid with a bow and arrows, the hunger diet (spiders, ant eggs, lizards, snakes, earth, wood), marsh roots, prickly-pear "tunas", and being forced to act as healers by prayer all follow his *Relación*.
- The arrowhead cut from a man's back near the heart and closed with two stitches is a real episode from his account. The "emerald" arrowheads (probably malachite or turquoise), the "land of cows" (bison) and the great river (the Rio Grande) are all from the account.
- The meeting with Diego de Alcaraz's slave hunters in 1536 is real. The four survivors were Cabeza de Vaca, Alonso del Castillo Maldonado, Andrés Dorantes and Estebanico. Estebanico came from Azemmour in Morocco and was Dorantes's slave, not a free Spanish soldier ("Esteban Nicomor" is a garble), but the show does include him.
- Calling him "the most humane of all the conquistadors" is a fair modern judgement. The sympathetic portrayal of Native peoples is better than the conquest stories common in the show's day.
