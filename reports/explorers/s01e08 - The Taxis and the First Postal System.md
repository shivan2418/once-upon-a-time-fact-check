# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e08: The Taxis and the First Postal System
(French episode.) The episode follows the Tassis (Taxis) family of couriers around 1490–1505. Riders who carry letters all the way themselves are slow, so the family sets up a chain of relay stations with fresh horses and local riders, signalled by a post horn. The system links Maximilian's court at Innsbruck with the Low Countries, and later Spain through France. The episode ends with the family's rise to princely rank and the 1867 sale of the Thurn und Taxis post to Prussia. It teaches how relay posts made Europe-wide communication fast and regular.

## Outdated (since 1996)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Normal, normal, avoue, tout semble normal, mais avant qu'on ne l'invente, il n'y avait pas de poste." [Normal, normal, admittedly everything seems normal, but before it was invented there was no post.]
  **Today:** Relay posts with fresh horses and riders were very old: the Persian *angarium*, the Roman *cursus publicus*, Chinese and Mongol (*yam*) relay systems, the Abbasid *barid*, and in France Louis XI's royal posts (1464/1479). The Tassis did not invent the relay. They organised the first large cross-border network run as a private business under contract, later open to private letters. The show's other episodes (Genghis Khan, Ibn Battuta) cover some of these earlier systems.
  **Why it was believed:** National postal histories and stamp-collecting literature celebrated the Taxis as "founders of the European post", and that slid into "inventors of the post".
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** major

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "Et le voyage va durer plus de deux mois." [And the voyage will last more than two months.]
  **Today:** Juana's fleet left Laredo on 22 August 1496 and reached Arnemuiden near Middelburg on about 8 September, after storms and a stop on the English coast. The trip took under three weeks.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Bon, chambelan, faites lettre de patente pour que les Tassis acheminent la poste en nos provinces de Castille et d'Aragon." [Well, chamberlain, draw up letters patent so that the Tassis carry the post in our provinces of Castile and Aragon.]
  **Today:** The Spanish post came to the Tassis from **Philip the Handsome**, not from Ferdinand or Isabella. Philip made Francisco de Tassis *Correo Mayor* of Castile on 18 January 1505, and Charles confirmed the post and passed it to Baptista de Tassis in 1516–18. The race between couriers is invented.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- Europe had roughly 80–100 million people around 1500.
- The claims that geography was "not taught" and that princes knew almost nothing of their lands are exaggerated, since Ptolemy's *Geography* was printed and studied from the 1470s. Maps did carry strategic value.
- Earlier letter carriers included merchants, monks and the "butchers' post" (*Metzgerpost*).
- The Tassis came from near Bergamo and served as papal couriers. Janetto, Francesco and Ruggiero worked for Frederick III and Maximilian from the late 1480s (the Innsbruck–Mechelen line dates from 1489–90). Johann Baptista (born 1470) is the nephew.
- The Burgundian marriage (Mary and Maximilian, 1477), Philip the Handsome's marriage to Juana, and the Habsburg union of Spain, Austria, Burgundy and the Low Countries are correct. Juana became heiress only in 1500.
- The route via Füssen and Ulm, relays every 7–10 leagues (about 30–40 km) with local riders, a post horn and a pouch, and running day and night are all accurate. Innsbruck/Brussels in about 5–6 days matches the 1505 contract.
- Juana's fleet of about 120–130 ships and 15,000–20,000 people is in the right range. Philip was away in Austria, and they married at once on meeting at Lier (October 1496). Maximilian was "King of the Romans" then, not yet Emperor.
- Charles (later Charles V) was born at Ghent in February 1500.
- The 1505 contract did run the Spain route through France, with Louis XII's agreement, via Paris and Blois. "Toledo–Brussels 12 days in summer, 14 in winter" is the contract's own figure.
- Maximilian raised the family to the nobility of the Empire (1512) and added the imperial eagle to their arms. "Père de l'Empire" is probably a mishearing.
- The family rose further: barons (1608), counts (1624) and princes (1695).
- In 1867 Thurn und Taxis ceded its posts to Prussia for about 3 million thalers.
