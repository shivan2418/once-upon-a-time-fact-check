# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e14: Bougainville and the Pacific
The episode opens with the Polynesian settlement of the Pacific, long before any European arrived. It then covers the European hunt for the imagined "great southern continent": Mendaña and Quirós for Spain, Tasman for the Dutch. Most of the episode follows Louis-Antoine de Bougainville's voyage round the world in 1766–69: the Strait of Magellan, Tahiti ("New Cythera"), Aotourou, the naturalist Commerson and Jeanne Baret, the first woman known to have sailed round the world.

## Outdated (since 1996)
- **Quote:** "In 400 AD, the Polynesians crossed 3,500 kilometers of ocean to settle in Hawaii."
  **Today:** Around 1996 the usual date for settling Hawaii was 300–500 AD (Kirch and others). Radiocarbon re-dating that kept only short-lived samples changed this (Wilmshurst et al., *PNAS* 2011). East Polynesia was settled late: the Society Islands around 1025–1120 AD, and Hawaii, New Zealand and Easter Island around 1190–1290 AD. The distance is roughly right: about 3,800 km from the Marquesas and 4,400 km from Tahiti.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "Others went as far as the island of Park, whose statues would be discovered 1,000 years later."
  **Today:** "Park" is a mishearing of *Pâques*, Easter Island (Rapa Nui). Dates around 1996 put its settlement at 400–800 AD. Hunt and Lipo (*Science* 2006) and the 2011 re-dating moved it to about 1150–1250 AD. Roggeveen arrived in 1722, so the gap is about 500 years, not 1,000.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "Well, not today, but it still existed two or three"
  **Today:** The giant bird is the moa, which lived only in New Zealand. Around 1996 popular books often had moa surviving until a few centuries ago. Holdaway and Jacomb (*Science* 2000) and later dating show the moa died out by about 1400–1450, within roughly 150 years of people arriving. That is more than 550 years ago. Australia had no moa: its giant birds (*Genyornis*, the dromornithids) died out tens of thousands of years ago, and the emu is still alive.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Almost 2,000 years ago, Ptolemy, the great geographer, had observed that most of the"
  **Today:** Ptolemy's maps did show an unknown southern land (*terra incognita*) closing off the Indian Ocean. However, the idea that a southern continent must "balance" the northern land masses comes from other Greek writers (Aristotle's climate zones, Crates of Mallus' four-continent globe) and above all from Renaissance cartographers such as Mercator. It is not Ptolemy's argument.
  **Why it was believed:** Popular histories of exploration routinely gave Ptolemy credit for the "balance" theory of *Terra Australis*.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "It was while stopping in Brazil that he discovered this plant"
  **Today:** The bougainvillea was collected at Rio de Janeiro in 1767 by the expedition's naturalist Philibert Commerson, probably helped by Jeanne Baret. Commerson named it after his commander. Bougainville did not discover it himself. Standard botanical references said so long before 1996.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- The Pacific covers about a third of Earth's surface, is about as large as all the other oceans combined (roughly 46% of the world ocean) and has well over 10,000 islands (estimates run to 20,000–30,000).
- "the pacific was already explored more than 4 000 years ago by the polynesians": Austronesian-speaking peoples left Taiwan about 5,000 years ago. Their Lapita descendants reached remote Oceania (Vanuatu, New Caledonia, Fiji, Tonga, Samoa) about 3,000–3,200 years ago, and a distinct Polynesian culture formed after that. To its credit, the show presents Polynesians as the true discoverers of the Pacific, ahead of any European.
- "The Solomon Islands, New Caledonia,": the main Solomon Islands had been settled more than 30,000 years earlier by earlier peoples (Kilu Cave, 1980s finds). The Lapita voyagers came much later. "Numea" (Nouméa) is a town in New Caledonia, not a separate island.
- Polynesian navigation by stars, winds, currents and migrating birds, and one-way voyages to settle new land, are accurately shown.
- "Magellan had already traveled around the world.": Magellan was killed in the Philippines in 1521. Elcano completed the voyage in 1522, as the show's own Magellan episode explains.
- Mendaña sailed from Peru in 1567 and found the Solomon Islands ("mendana spotted a few islands"; he was the nephew of the governor, Lope García de Castro). His 1595 voyage had Quirós as pilot. Quirós then led the 1605–06 voyage to Espiritu Santo in Vanuatu (the New Hebrides), which he took to be the southern continent.
- Tasman in 1642: his carpenter really did swim through the surf to plant the flag in Van Diemen's Land, an anecdote the show gets right. Nobody knew it was an island ("The land that Tasman discovered was an island south of Australia called Tasmania.") until Bass and Flinders in 1798.
- New Zealand: "but he thought it wiser to stay away from it." In Golden Bay, Māori killed four of Tasman's men, and he left without landing. The episode plays this down.
- Bougainville was a lawyer, the author of a treatise on integral calculus, a Fellow of the Royal Society (1756), a musketeer, Montcalm's aide-de-camp in Canada, and a Knight of Saint-Louis. "Louis X" is a mishearing of Louis XV.
- "the minister, well known for his wars, replied to you with morgue.": the "house on fire / stables" retort was made by the naval minister Berryer ("wars" is a mishearing) in 1758–59, when Bougainville asked for reinforcements for Canada. The episode wraps a proposed attack on the Carolinas into the same scene.
- "There was your conquest of the" Malouines: Bougainville settled the Falklands at Port Saint-Louis in 1764, and France handed the colony to Spain in 1767.
- Pierre-Antoine Véron, astronomer; the frigate *La Boudeuse*, built near Nantes (Indret); Choiseul as minister; Commerson; and the Prince of Nassau-Siegen all match the sources. The supply ship *Étoile* is left out.
- About 52 days through the Strait of Magellan, the Tuamotus, and Tahiti named "Nouvelle-Cythère" (April 1768, a stay of about 10 days). Wallis had reached Tahiti in 1767, and the show does not claim Bougainville was first.
- Trading for nails, the killing of islanders by French soldiers (Bougainville put the suspects in chains), Aotourou (Ahutoru, brother of the chief Ereti) going to France, and the claim of possession for Louis XV are all accurate.
- Jeanne Baret disguised as Commerson's valet is accurate. "You will be the first woman to have circumnavigated the world.": she is the first woman known to have done it, but she left the ship with Commerson on Mauritius in 1768 and finished the trip only around 1775.
- "Bouinville narrowly avoided the coral reef that runs along Australia.": in June 1768 he hit the outer Great Barrier Reef and turned north. He returned home by New Guinea, the Moluccas, Batavia, Mauritius and the Cape, and reached France in March 1769, about 2 years and 4 months after leaving.
