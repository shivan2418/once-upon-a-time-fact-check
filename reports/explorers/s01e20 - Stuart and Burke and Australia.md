# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e20: Stuart and Burke and Australia
The episode opens with Cook at Botany Bay in 1770 and reminds viewers that Aboriginal people reached Australia tens of thousands of years earlier. It then moves through the convict colony at Sydney, Matthew Flinders and the name "Australia", and early inland explorers such as Leichhardt and Kennedy. Most of the episode is the 1860–62 race to cross the continent from south to north: Burke and Wills's Victorian expedition, with its camels, the Dig Tree and the deaths, and John McDouall Stuart's successful South Australian crossing. It shows Aboriginal people as watchers, victims, guides and rescuers.

## Outdated (since 1996)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Australia was connected to New Guinea and Indonesia."
  **Today:** At low sea levels Australia, New Guinea and Tasmania formed one landmass (Sahul), and western Indonesia joined mainland Asia (Sunda). Between the two lay the deep-water islands of Wallacea, so even at the lowest sea levels the first Australians had to make several sea crossings, at least one of about 70–90 km. The first settlers came by boat or raft, not "on foot", which makes them some of the earliest known seafarers. Geologists and archaeologists knew this well before 1996 (Wallace's Line; Birdsell, 1977).
  **Why it was believed:** "They walked across a land bridge" was the easy popular version, borrowed from the Bering land bridge story.
  **Confidence:** high

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "I take possession of this land in the name of England."
  **Today:** Cook did not claim the land at Botany Bay (April–May 1770). He claimed the whole east coast for King George III, in the name of Great Britain rather than "England", at Possession Island off Cape York on 22 August 1770.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Governor, I have explored New Zealand. It is an enormous island, like a continent."
  **Today:** Flinders charted Australia, not New Zealand. His circumnavigation in 1801–03 showed that New Holland and New South Wales were one continent. He promoted the name "Australia" in his 1814 book, and Governor Macquarie took it up from 1817. This may be a dubbing or transcription slip for "New Holland".
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "The beginning of the 19th century, only a few ports and small towns were founded"
  **Today:** Of the towns listed, only Sydney (1788) and Hobart (1804) existed in the early 1800s. Brisbane (1824), Albany (1826), Perth (1829), Melbourne (1835) and Adelaide (1836) came later. Darwin (founded as Palmerston in 1869) was settled only after Stuart's crossing, so the later line "it'll take us to the port of Darwin" is anachronistic too. The harbour had been named Port Darwin in 1839, but there was no town.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Another explorer, John Septimborough, also tried."
  **Today:** This is surely John Septimus Roe, Surveyor-General of Western Australia, and he did have 13 children. His 1848–49 expedition went from the Avon valley to the Russell Range and the south coast inside Western Australia. It was not an attempt to cross the continent.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "It's been five months since they left and we're still waiting."
  **Today:** Burke left Cooper Creek on 16 December 1860. William Brahe, told to wait three months, held on for just over four and left on the morning of 21 April 1861, the day Burke, Wills and King got back.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Thus, on July 25, 1862, two explorers managed"
  **Today:** Stuart's party reached the sea at Chambers Bay, Van Diemen Gulf, on 24 July 1862. All ten men of the party, not two, made it there and back alive. If "two explorers" means Burke and Wills, they reached the tidal Flinders/Bynoe estuary in February 1861 and are usually credited with the first south–north crossing, though they died on the way back.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- Opening by saying that Australia "was discovered before captain Cook", by its first peoples, is better than the "Cook discovered Australia" myth common in 1990s children's media. Throughout, the show gives Aboriginal people a voice: worries about food and sheep, defending their land, guiding to water, giving fish and nardoo.
- About 50,000 years for the first arrival matched the 1990s evidence. Current estimates run from about 50,000 to 65,000 years (the Madjedbebe dates of 2017 are still debated). Tasmania and New Guinea were joined to Australia at low sea level.
- Botany Bay was named for Banks and Solander's plant collection.
- Pitt's government and Lord Sydney (Home Secretary) sent convicts after American independence closed off transportation there. Arthur Phillip was the first governor. In fact he rejected Botany Bay and named Sydney Cove in Port Jackson after Lord Sydney (January 1788).
- "The Darwin, the Murray" is probably a mishearing of the Darling and the Murray, which Charles Sturt traced in 1828–30. Hume and Hovell had reached the upper Murray in 1824.
- Leichhardt vanished in 1848 trying to cross from east to west. Nobody knows his fate, and a flood is only one theory. Edmund Kennedy was speared near Cape York in December 1848 after earlier clashes, on an expedition to Cape York rather than a crossing.
- "No one has ever managed to cross it", said around 1859, refers to the centre. Eyre had gone along the south coast from east to west (1841), and Leichhardt from Moreton Bay to Port Essington (1844–45).
- South Australia offered a £2,000 reward for the first south–north crossing. Victoria answered with the costly Burke and Wills expedition rather than a bigger reward.
- William Kekwick went with Stuart. Burke was appointed months before leaving Melbourne in August 1860. The camels came from India via Karachi with cameleers loosely called "Afghans". The party was slowed by its wagons and stores, built a stockade at Cooper Creek, and split there. Burke, Wills, King and Gray went north, and only Burke and Wills reached tidal salt water, having left the camels behind. Mangroves kept them from seeing the open sea.
- The "DIG" tree, the stores buried 3 ft north-west of it, Brahe's party leaving the same morning, the push towards Mount Hopeless, and eating nardoo seed are all accurate. King survived and was found by Howitt in September 1861. The show omits that the Yandruwandha people kept him alive.
- Stuart was turned back at Attack Creek in 1860 after a clash with Warumungu men. On his 1861–62 expedition he followed the Adelaide River valley to the north coast. The Stuart Highway follows his route roughly.
