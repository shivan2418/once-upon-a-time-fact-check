# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e26: Up to the Stars
The series finale steps back from exploration to recap the whole "Once Upon a Time" saga: the Big Bang, the evolution of life and humans, great inventions and the explorers of earlier episodes. It then turns to warnings about pollution, population growth and nuclear waste, and uses a 12-hour clock to show how recent humans are. It ends looking outward: the first exoplanets around Sun-like stars (51 Pegasi, 1995), ENIAC and Hubble, interstellar distances and speculation about light-speed travel. The message is that there is always more to explore, but we have only one Earth to look after.

## Outdated (since 1996)
- **Quote:** "That's about 15 billion years."
  **Today:** In 1996 the age of the universe was estimated at 10–20 billion years, and 15 was the usual round figure. WMAP (2003) and Planck (2013–2018) fixed it at 13.8 billion years. That also changes the episode's "another 10 billion years" to form the Earth: it is about 9.2 billion.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "Pluto, was discovered just a few decades ago."
  **Today:** Pluto was found in 1930, which was correct. In 2006 the IAU reclassified it as a dwarf planet, so the outermost planet is now Neptune.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "take the Orion Nebula. It's 1,500 light years away."
  **Today:** About 1,500–1,600 light years was the standard figure in 1996. VLBA and VERA radio parallaxes (2007–2008) measured about 1,350 light years (about 414 pc).
  **Confidence:** medium

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "Neanderthal, and Cro-Magnon, Modern Man."
  **Today:** The list of hominins is a single "march of progress" ladder leading to Cro-Magnon. Neanderthals were a sister lineage, not our ancestors. "Robustus" (Paranthropus) was a dead-end side branch. Human evolution is a branching bush. (Most names in the list are mishearings, e.g. "Homoilus" for Homo habilis.)
  **Why it was believed:** The ladder image was everywhere in popular books and museum posters, even though palaeoanthropologists described a bush by the 1980s. Neanderthal mtDNA (1997) settled the point for the public.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "At 11.40, the end of the dinosaurs and the appearance of small rodents."
  **Today:** Mammals did not appear when the dinosaurs died out. Small mammals had lived alongside them for about 150 million years; the "Triconodon" in the episode's list is itself a Jurassic mammal. Rodents appeared only after the extinction and are not on the line leading to humans.
  **Why it was believed:** "The dinosaurs died and the little mammals took over" was the standard textbook shorthand. Mesozoic mammals were well known to specialists.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Man has 12 hours left, or nearly 5 billion years, to spend on Earth."
  **Today:** The Sun will leave the main sequence in about 5 billion years. But it slowly brightens, and models (e.g. Caldeira & Kasting, 1992) give Earth about 1 billion years before the oceans are lost and complex life ends.
  **Why it was believed:** "The Sun has 5 billion years left" was the usual popular figure. The shorter habitability window had not reached general books.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Gregors and Phoenicians circumnavigated Africa"
  **Today:** The only source for the Phoenician voyage round Africa for Pharaoh Necho II (about 600 BC) is a single report in Herodotus, and historians are still divided on it. "Reached the polar regions" overstates Pytheas, who reached "Thule" near the Arctic Circle in the 4th century BC. ("Gregors" = Greeks.)
  **Why it was believed:** Popular histories of exploration often told Necho's circumnavigation as fact, citing Herodotus's detail about the sun being on the "wrong" side.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Then came the computer. The first computer."
  **Today:** ENIAC (1945–46, built by John Mauchly and J. Presper Eckert, the two "Johns" of the gag) was not the first computer. Zuse's Z3 (1941), the Atanasoff–Berry Computer (1942) and the British Colossus (1943–44) came earlier. A 1973 US court ruling even invalidated the ENIAC patent and credited Atanasoff. The figures given are mostly right (about 17,500 tubes, 1,500 relays, about 6,000 switches), but it had about 70,000 resistors, not 50,000.
  **Why it was believed:** ENIAC was the standard "first computer" in popular books. Colossus was secret until the mid-1970s.
  **Confidence:** medium

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "On our watch, it is then 3.40, the first mollusks at 8 o'clock"
  **Today:** On a 12-hour clock where 12 hours = 5 billion years, one hour is about 417 million years and one minute about 7 million years. Several times are badly off. Molluscs (about 540 million years ago) belong at about 10:42, not 8:00, which would be 1.7 billion years ago. Dinosaurs ("at 11 o'clock", about 417 million years ago) appeared about 230 million years ago, at about 11:27. Their extinction (66 million years ago) is at about 11:50, not 11:40. Australopithecus (about 4 million years ago) is at about 11:59:25, not 11:55, which would be about 35 million years ago. Modern humans (about 0.2–0.3 million years ago) are in the last 2–3 seconds, not at 11:59. The first-cell time (3:40) is reasonable.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "Another 32 million centuries for Homo erectus to appear."
  **Today:** The demography timeline adds up to Homo erectus about 300 million years ago (5 billion − 1.5 billion − 3.2 billion years) and Cro-Magnon "another 30 million centuries" later, which is impossible. Homo erectus appeared about 1.9 million years ago and Cro-Magnon about 40,000 years ago. The units may have been mangled in translation (e.g. thousand and million centuries).
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Statistics predict 8 billion people in 2010"
  **Today:** No statistical agency predicted this. The episode simply keeps doubling every 35 years up to "128 billion in 2150". The UN's 1994 projections gave about 6.9 billion for 2010 and about 9.4 billion for 2050, levelling off at about 11 billion. The real 2010 figure was about 6.9 billion, and 8 billion was reached in November 2022.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "rivers dump a ton of waste per capita per day into the sea."
  **Today:** Even in industrialised countries, household solid waste was about 1–2 kg per person per day, and domestic wastewater a few hundred litres. A tonne of waste per person per day is far too high unless it includes all industrial and cooling water. The figure has no clear source.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Hey, there's another message. French astronauts have"
  **Today:** The next planets after 51 Pegasi b were found in 1996 by the American astronomers Geoffrey Marcy and Paul Butler: 70 Virginis, 47 Ursae Majoris and tau Boötis. Tau Boötis ("the star Boris", about 51 light years away) fits the episode's "52 light years". They were not French, and they were astronomers, not astronauts. This may be a dub slip.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "Pegas 51 in sight."
  **Today:** The closing scene of a spaceship reaching 51 Pegasi to find out "if it is habitable" ignores what was announced in 1995. 51 Pegasi b is a gas giant of about half a Jupiter mass, orbiting its star every 4.2 days at about 0.05 AU, at over 1,000 °C. It was the first "hot Jupiter", and nobody thought it could be habitable.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "From Belgium to Austria in three days."
  **Today:** Under the 1505 Taxis contract, post from Brussels to Innsbruck (about 920 km) took about 5½ days in summer and 6½ in winter. That was still remarkably fast, but not three days.
  **Confidence:** medium
- **Quote:** "black holes. All this is far too complicated for me."
  **Today:** The folded-balloon shortcut between two distant points is a wormhole (named by Wheeler in 1957), not a black hole. Black holes are not tunnels or shortcuts.
  **Confidence:** high
- **Quote:** "the matter-antimatter ships that would travel at the speed of light."
  **Today:** Under special relativity nothing with mass can reach the speed of light, however it is powered. Antimatter propulsion could in theory reach a good fraction of it. The "never say never" framing presents a physical law as a technical obstacle. (The 1 billion km/h conversion is correct.)
  **Confidence:** medium

## Simplified but fine
- The Big Bang at "billions of degrees" and "we descend from stardust" are fine.
- First life within about 1–1.5 billion years of Earth's formation and the first tetrapods about 365 million years ago (about 4.2 billion years after formation) are fine. "Amoeba" stands loosely for the first cells.
- In 1996, "probably a meteorite" for the dinosaur extinction was the right level of caution (Alvarez, 1980; Chicxulub, 1991).
- "Darwin, man descends from the [ape]" is the usual shorthand for common ancestry. Galileo championed rather than originated the heliocentric view. Edison improved the telegraph but did not invent it.
- The explorer recap is consistent with the series: Vikings in America before Columbus, Zheng He ("Zong") to Africa. "Columbus and the Pinzón brothers discovered America" uses the usual European framing, softened by naming the Vikings first. "Tyler and Tencel at the summit of the Vrest" is a mishearing of Hillary and Tenzing on Everest.
- World population: about 250 million in AD 1, about 500 million in 1500, about 4 billion in 1975 are all fine. "2 billion" four centuries after 1500 is a bit high: there were about 1.6 billion in 1900, and 2 billion was reached in about 1927.
- Deep geological disposal of nuclear waste in clay or granite was and is the main option, as in France's Bure clay laboratory. "150 million years" is roughly ten half-lives of iodine-129 (15.7 million years), though most of the radiotoxicity decays within 100,000 years to 1 million years.
- Mayor and Queloz (Geneva) announced 51 Pegasi b on 6 October 1995 using radial-velocity "wobble", correctly explained with the ball on a string. The planet orbits 51 Pegasi; it is not a planet in Pegasus itself. "45 light years" matches 1995 press figures, and Hipparcos later gave about 50. They won the 2019 Nobel Prize. Earlier pulsar planets (1992) go unmentioned.
- "It all started with Marconi" is a loose link: 51 Pegasi b was found by optical spectroscopy, not radio.
- Hubble at about 600 km, flawed mirror, repaired in space: the mirror was ground to the wrong shape rather than "warped", and it was fixed by the 1993 servicing mission.
- Alpha Centauri at about 4.3 light years ≈ 40,000 billion km. At 100,000 km/h the trip takes about 45,000 years, and the star is about 11 times closer than 51 Pegasi.
- A low orbit circles the Earth in about 90 minutes. Light at 300,000 km/s covers Paris–Beijing in about 0.03 s.
- 5 billion years to roughly 100 years ("50 million times less time") for industrial impact is internally consistent.
