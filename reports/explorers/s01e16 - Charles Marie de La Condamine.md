# Once Upon a Time... The Explorers — s01e16: Charles Marie de La Condamine
The episode starts with the 18th-century argument over the Earth's shape: Newton said it was flattened at the poles, Cassini said it was stretched. It then follows the French Academy's 1735 expedition to the equator near Quito. Along the way it shows La Condamine's trip up the Esmeraldas river with Pedro Vicente Maldonado, the hardships and quarrels of the survey, his descent of the Amazon in 1743, and his report on rubber, curare and Amazonian nature to the Paris Academy.

## Outdated (since 1996)
None found.

## Common belief at the time
- **Quote:** "He revealed a new world that till then was totally unknown!"
  **Today:** The Amazon was not unknown. Orellana went down it in 1541–42 and Pedro Teixeira went up and back in 1637–39 (Acuña published an account in 1641). Jesuit missionaries such as Samuel Fritz had mapped it, and the episode itself mentions Orellana and Fritz. Many Indigenous peoples lived there. La Condamine's real contribution was the first scientific account and map, with measured positions, written for European science.
  **Why it was believed:** Popular accounts often described Europe's scientific "discovery" as revealing a land nobody knew.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Wrong (even in 1996)
- **Quote:** "a most timely gesture: 100,000 pounds sterling."
  **Today:** La Condamine is said to have spent about 100,000 *livres* of his own money on the expedition. The livre was a French currency, so "pounds sterling" is a dubbing error: *livres* means "pounds", but not British money.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "Monsieur Couplet, clockmaker and mechanic"
  **Today:** The instrument-maker and clockmaker was Théodore Hugot. Couplet (Couplet-Viguier) was a young assistant, the nephew of an academician, who died of fever soon after arriving in 1736. "Captain Vergard" is probably Jean-Joseph Verguin, a naval engineer and draftsman.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "About 50 years ago, the survivors founded the village on this river, the Esmeralda."
  **Today:** The free Afro-Indigenous communities of Esmeraldas began with a slave ship wrecked in 1553, led by Alonso de Illescas. That was about 180 years before La Condamine came in 1736, not 50.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor
- **Quote:** "His nephew, Pedro Maldonado, will go too."
  **Today:** Pedro Vicente Maldonado was a scientist and official born in Riobamba. He was not the nephew of the Jesuit Samuel Fritz, who was Bohemian. He did go down the Amazon in 1743, meeting La Condamine on the way, and later went to Europe, where he died in London in 1748.
  **Confidence:** medium
  **Severity:** minor

## Simplified but fine
- "Like great English style, they're much flatter around the poles, more like a potato!" and "But the French astronomer, Cassini, believed the contrary.": Newton's oblate Earth against the Cassinis' prolate one is accurate, and Voltaire did back Newton.
- "Another expedition to the North Pole will do the same.": Maupertuis worked in Lapland, in the Tornio valley at about 66°N, near the Arctic Circle and far from the pole. The show later gets this right ("Laplante").
- The expedition's official leader was Louis Godin. The show puts La Condamine at the centre, which suits the episode, and gives the rest of the team (Bouguer, Jean Godin des Odonais, Jussieu, Seniergues, Morainville) correctly. The Spanish officers Jorge Juan and Antonio de Ulloa are left out.
- "Quito, the ancient capital of Peru is here to the northwest": Quito belonged to the Viceroyalty of Peru and had been a northern Inca capital. The expedition landed at Manta in 1736, and La Condamine went up the Esmeraldas while the others went by Guayaquil.
- Latex and waterproof cloth at Esmeraldas, rope bridges, and suspicious colonial officials (spying and treasure rumours, lawsuits) all match La Condamine's own account.
- Seniergues was killed by a crowd at a bullfight in Cuenca in 1739 after a quarrel over a woman, and Diego de León was involved. Jean Godin married Isabel Gramesón ("Sir, will you give me your daughter's hand in marriage?"). Louis Godin took a post in Lima ("It happens, I have been offered a post of just an astronomer in Lima, in Peru.") and later ran the naval academy at Cádiz.
- Bouguer and La Condamine quarrelled and raced to publish, and Bouguer got back to Paris first (1744).
- "200 years ago spaniard orellana went down that great river": 1541–42 is right, and so is the Amazons legend from Carvajal's chronicle. "Father Fritz" (Samuel Fritz) published his map in 1707.
- "I mean, curare is a deadly poison, but we can consume animals killed with it, and there is no danger.": this is correct. La Condamine brought curare back and tested it.
- "How strange! Two rivers flowing down the same riverbed.": the meeting of the dark Rio Negro and the muddy Amazon is accurate. "We are coming to Manaus." uses a later name: the place was then a small fort, São José da Barra do Rio Negro, and was called Manaus only from 1856.
- "It's from the tribe of women warriors.": La Condamine really did collect stories of green "Amazon stones" (*muiraquitã*), and the show leaves the question open, as he did.
- "When it is processed using heat, it can be molded into any shape.": raw rubber goes sticky in heat and brittle in cold. Rubber that keeps its shape needed vulcanization (Goodyear, 1839). The tree was named *Hevea* only in 1775 (Aublet), and Fresneau described it in French Guiana.
- "Oh, I mean new Amazonia, especially the Amazon from its source, down to the sea, 4,000 miles.": about 6,400 km, which is right. "A giant of a river, 200,000 cubic meters of water every second, and deposits sediment hundreds of miles out in the ocean.": also right (about 209,000 m³/s on average). La Condamine started from Jaén on the Marañón, not from the source.
